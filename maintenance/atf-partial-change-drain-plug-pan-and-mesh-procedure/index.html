    AKPP partial ATF change — drain plug, pan, mesh

    Tags: #automatic-transmission · #atf · #drain-plug

    The routine partial fluid change: draining through the bottom plug, optionally dropping the pan to clean the mesh, and refilling.

    Procedure — bottom drain / partial change (the routine method). Warm the transmission to 40–50°C, then unscrew the lower drain plug — a thin, steady stream indicates correct level; if insufficient, add fluid via the drain hole using a technical syringe. Do not check at high temperature (~80°C) — flow is excessive and the reading is inaccurate (though checking near idle temperature ~90°C is also accepted practice by some in the community on service visits — this is a documented disagreement).123456789101112 A partial change at 60,000 km typically drains ~4.5 L, replaces the filter, and refills; a full/complete drain-refill was historically done at 120,000 km. Some owners do partial changes earlier, at 50,000 km, for extended oil life. Draining through the dipstick tube with a hand pump is convenient when no service pit is available. Warning, strongly emphasized by the community: never completely drain the AKPP multiple times in a row — this destroys internal transmission components; partial changes can safely be repeated at 20,000–40,000 km intervals.131415161718192021

    Simple pan-plug-only method, no filter access (2025-02 report). For a straightforward partial change without dropping the pan: drain fluid from the bottom drain plug (easy to identify); measure the drained amount using a tube positioned below the drain hole to track level; refill through the side fill bolt near the front of the transmission using a syringe. Multiple owners confirmed this method works well for routine partial fluid changes.2223242526

    Partial exchange procedure — mesh filter cleaning and refill methods (2025-06 report, expands on the routine method above). Recommended over a full drain-and-fill: (1) Drain through the bottom pan plug — without removing the pan, roughly 2.5 L drains; with the pan removed, roughly 4 L drains. (2) While the pan is off, check the coarse metal mesh filter inside it and wash it with gasoline, kerosene, or carburetor cleaner. (3) Replace the pan gasket — old gaskets dry out and start leaking. (4) Refill either via the top filler near the heat exchanger, or via the drain hole using a syringe or gravity-fed container (never attempt gravity feed without a supporting container — it takes far too long otherwise). (5) Repeat the partial-exchange cycle 2–3 times total for the best overall fluid turnover. Total system capacity with the cooler is approximately 7.4 L; the base transmission alone holds approximately 6 L.272829303132333435363738394041424344454647

    Fluid check and partial replacement without a dipstick (community procedure). The X80 has no transmission dipstick — fluid level is checked by removing the drain plug at the bottom of the pan: fluid flowing out steadily indicates a correct level. A partial replacement uses about 4 liters of fluid; replace the pan gasket during the service. While the pan is off, remove and inspect/clean the transmission filter element (steel mesh) and the interior of the pan for metal debris and magnetic contamination. Don't replace the drain-plug bolts if they haven't seized — reuse them with light lubrication. One reported case: an automatic-transmission car at 91,000 km with a history of jerking on 1-2-3 shifts — the recommended approach is to check the fluid level first, then proceed to a full fluid change if it's found low or dirty.4849505152535455565758

    50,000+ km used-car service procedure, restated (2024-08 report). For a used X80 at 50,000+ km: perform a partial (semi-flush) change first as a baseline. On the first pan-removal service (recommended for higher-mileage cars), clean the strainer thoroughly, install a new pan gasket (35168-TFA050), and refill with 4L (no pan removal) or 4–4.2L (with pan removal). ATF must carry the 3309 spec or Toyota TYPE IV equivalent — do not mix green and red fluids. Check used-car service history skeptically, since records may be incomplete.596061
