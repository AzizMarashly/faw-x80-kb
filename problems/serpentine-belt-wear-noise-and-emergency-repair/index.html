    Serpentine belt wear noise and emergency repair

    Tags: #cold-start · #winter

    Wear noise at 128,000 km. At 128,000 km, one owner's serpentine belt showed wear symptoms: early-morning squeaking/crackling before the engine warms up12. Note: visible factory fibers on the belt surface are normal residue from production and are not by themselves a wear indicator34. However, rotating the belt by hand and hearing internal cracking noise does suggest a need for replacement, even if the belt doesn't look badly worn from the outside5678.

    Emergency roadside repair when the belt breaks. In extreme weather (one user experienced −18°C overnight on a highway), a broken serpentine belt during travel can be temporarily worked around. Documented example: the user used clothing items (stockings/pantyhose) as an emergency belt substitute to restore enough engine function to reach shelter91011. While not a real fix, such emergency measures can let the vehicle limp to the nearest service location in a critical situation — treat it strictly as a get-you-home measure, not a repair1213.

    Whistle on cold, damp mornings at about 50,000 km. One owner hears a whistle for about 30 seconds after start and blames the drive belt or a roller; it sounds like an old Zhiguli14. A second owner heard a squeak on warm-up at 50,000 km and suspected the release (clutch) bearing, though he was unsure whether it came from the gearbox or the belt15. Victor believes that at this mileage neither the belt nor the roller should need replacing16. For a similar cold-start sound traced to the transmission side see cold-start whistling from the transmission side.
