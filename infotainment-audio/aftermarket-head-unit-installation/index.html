    Aftermarket tablet head unit installation

    Tags: #head-unit · #radio · #wiring · #infotainment · #price

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Installing a tablet-format aftermarket head unit (car radio) on the X80 requires: - A CANBUS adapter cable specific to the FAW X80 2018+.12345 - Removal of the factory radio frame/bezel and wiring-harness adaptation.36

    Challenges reported: - Factory wiring is poorly organized and too short — the harness had to be unclipped from its fixing clips in multiple locations on the panel to accommodate the new unit.36 - Antenna connector incompatibility — the factory radio antenna cable on the X80 is very short; an extension cable is required. Two Ozon extension-cable listings were confirmed to fit by different owners.78910 - Internal microphone support — the factory design may not support using a head unit's built-in mic; an external microphone connection is needed instead.56111213

    Installation time: roughly 1–2 hours for an experienced installer, significantly longer for a first attempt.341112

    Result reported: once correctly configured (CANBUS setup, antenna connection, external mic), everything works without issues — better music quality/volume, responsive navigation, and climate/rear-camera integration functioning correctly.24513

    Stock radio removal time (2024-06 report). Removing the factory head unit from the dashboard takes roughly 20–30 minutes, with the main risk being breaking the plastic trim clips that hold the surrounding panels — go slowly and avoid excess force. One owner needed a full extra teardown-and-reassembly pass because a USB cable was forgotten during the first attempt, adding another 10–15 minutes. Multiple owners report that despite the reassembly hassle, the sound-quality/feature upgrade from a new aftermarket unit is worth it, even through the factory speakers.141516171819

    Teyes CC3 2K all-in-one unit — components and total cost (2024-06 report, RU market). A complete stereo upgrade using the Teyes CC3 2K (10-inch QLED screen, 8-core processor, DSP, 4G modem, voice control, 2-DIN form factor, optional 360° camera) costs 33,000–40,000 RUB with active discounts (list price 40,000+ RUB without discounts), including a 1-year warranty. Required components beyond the head unit itself: a transition/adapter frame, a wiring harness, and optionally a camera. Buying the frame, harness, and unit separately is significantly cheaper than a bundled kit — one owner's frame purchase from Ozon ran 15,000–25,000 RUB (June 2024, RU market) cheaper via that route versus AliExpress alternatives for the same part, and another owner reported paying ~33,000 RUB total for everything combined by buying on active sales/discounts (vs. 40,000+ RUB at full price, and up to ~49,000 RUB at the high end; June 2024, RU market).2021222324252627282930

    Owner's voice report on a head-unit install. An owner who fitted an aftermarket head unit says the trim clips were hard to free, and the wires were too short and had to be routed up into a free space; afterwards the climate and door-open indicators and the rear camera worked. The unit wants an external microphone: the built-in mic performs worse and causes noise. She later reports better sound, a fine navigation experience and a good frame around the display 3113.
