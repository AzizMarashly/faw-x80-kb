    Engine oil — spec, viscosity, and brands

    Tags: #engine-oil

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    This page includes Syrian-market information.

    Official spec (CA4GD1 gasoline engine). The manual specifies 5W-30 as the primary motor oil for the CA4GD1 engine, with 5W-20 and 5W-40 as acceptable alternatives depending on ambient temperature (chart range -15°C to +45°C)12. The manual warns against using higher-viscosity oil at low temperatures, as it can cause starting difficulty3. A later manual excerpt confirms: quality grade SM minimum, viscosity 5W-40 / 0W-20 / 5W-30, choose per ambient temperature456. One owner (at ~150,000 km mileage) flagged a nuance worth checking directly against the formal approvals document: despite 5W-40 appearing on general viscosity charts, they found only 5W-30 actually listed under the official approvals ("допуски"), with no 5W-40 entry78.

    Oil capacity. 4.3L total with a filter change, or 3.9L for an oil-only change (no filter)49.

    API standard hierarchy. For reference, API oil-quality letters improve in this order: SL → SN → SM → SP, with SP being the highest/most current standard as of 20241011. ZIC X9 FE is confirmed usable on the X8012. The engine's minimum requirement is API SM13.

    Brand experience reported by owners: - VMPAVTO 5W-30 (synthetic) and TOTACHI 5W-30 (synthetic) — both available on Ozon, good reported performance1415. - Valvoline 5W-40, Repsoil 5W-40, Mobil 5W-40 — also used successfully1617. - Counterfeit warning: imported oil brands are frequently counterfeited; buy only from authorized dealers or brand websites181920. - Lukoil 5W-40 used with no wear issues reported at 55,000 km21. Lukoil 5W-30 also acceptable and reported to give smoother engine operation than 5W-4022. - MANN FILTER W71273 recommended as the oil filter to pair with these oils, specifically for X80 automatic-transmission cars23. - Prices vary by region/store; some shops charge ~500 RUB (RU market, Sept 2024) more for premium brand oil vs. generic2425. - Mitasu 5W-30 SM/CF fully synthetic (King Size 5L bottle) and Valvoline SynPower 5W-40 also used as a Mobil alternative2627. - Base-oil sourcing discussion: community view is that base motor oil commonly comes from a small number (~5-6) of global manufacturers (Lukoil among them), and many "branded" oils (Motul, Idemitsu, etc.) add proprietary additive packages on top of the same base stock282930. One owner adds that Toyota, like most automakers, doesn't manufacture its own oil either — the base could well be Lukoil, blended with additives to the automaker's spec31. As a concrete example, Lukoil's Genesis Armotech was reportedly supplied in Mercedes-branded canisters around 2014-2017, because Mercedes wanted an inexpensive but quality-consistent oil32. The same commenter states that essentially all cars assembled in Russia are filled with Lukoil at the factory, since it's the cheapest/most accessible option for automakers operating there33. On "Japanese" oil brands specifically: the claim is that no genuinely Japanese-made oil is sold in Russia — brands like Idemitsu etc. are not actually shipped from Japan34; similarly, European oil sold in tin canisters is suspect, since storing/selling oil in tin containers is prohibited within the EU itself35. A counter-voice notes Japan itself reportedly has only ~1-2 actual oil producers, with other "Japanese" brands merely registered there while relying on subcontracted base stock36. Counterfeit/low-quality oil is common in the market; poor packaging quality is cited as a tell3738. Lukoil is confirmed as the factory (OEM) fill for Russian-market assembly3940. - Mobil-branded oil reported to extend the practical drain interval: one owner's family car showed reduced oil consumption and could go 8,000 km between changes on Mobil, vs. 4,000-6,000 km on other brands4142. Recommendation: buy from brand-name gas stations to reduce counterfeit risk434445. - GENESIS SPECIAL SPX 5W-40 also reported used for an oil change (with genuine FAW filters and an X40-sourced drain-plug washer), alongside a wheel alignment done at the same service visit; owner reported the car ran more responsively afterward46.

    Viscosity and mileage. At ~130,000 km on 5W-30, one owner noticed increased oil consumption under spirited driving; switching to 5W-40 was recommended, as the heavier film holds up better in a worn engine4748. That owner's car had a factory fill (at ~85,000 km) of 5W-40 branded "Luckily" with no consumption issues reported at that mileage4950. General guidance: oil-grade choice should track engine wear — higher-mileage engines often do better on the heavier grade515253.

    Recommended viscosity restated, plus a dealership case. Recommended motor oils are 5W-30 or 5W-40 with SM, SN, SP certification or higher. One owner reported using GENESIS SPECIAL SPX 5W-40 at an official dealership, at 86,000 km on the odometer, with good results5455.

    A3/B4 vs A5/B5 classification preference. The manual specifies A3/B4 classification, but owners also report using A5/B5 (lower-viscosity oils, e.g. Miraks 0W-30). One owner (Vasily) recommends Teboil Diamond Carat 5W-30 (A3/B4); another (Victor) uses Syntec 9000 5W-30 A3/B4. Stated preference in the thread: A5/B5 if available, A3/B4 otherwise acceptable56575859.

    API SN and ACEA A3/B4 restated as the practical recommendation. A separate, later exchange restates the recommended oil classification as API SN and ACEA A3/B4. Higher specs (A5/B5) are noted as more closely aligned with Mazda's own factory specification (consistent with the shared engine lineage), but are not strictly required. Oils meeting the SN/A3B4 combination are confirmed working in the community6061.

    Duratec HE oil spec — WSS-M2-C913-C, and brand recommendations. The Duratec HE engine's specified oil standard is WSS-M2-C913-C. Recommended oils meeting or matching it: ROLF GT 5W-30 (made in Russia, Obninsk — the same facility that produces Syntec- and Tatachи-branded oils), Taif Allegro, and Taif Forte (stated to exactly match the WSS-M2-C913-C spec). The engine is described as tolerant: any correctly-viscosity, correctly-volume motor oil will technically work, but matching the spec is preferred. ROLF GT 5W-30 is available on Ozon626364656667686970717273.

    Factory-approved viscosity grades — restated, with a caution on 10W grades. The manual specifies 0W-20, 5W-30, and 5W-40. Community consensus favors 5W-30 as the standard per-manual choice, with 5W-40 acceptable for summer driving. 10W-40 is not factory-approved; 10W-30 is acceptable for winter use but not recommended for regular use otherwise. Synthetic/semi-synthetic brands mentioned: Rosneft Magnum Ultratec 5W-40, Sintec ExtraLife 7000 5W-40, Mobil, and Lukoil. Change intervals: 5,000-10,000 km depending on city vs. highway use. One caution: frequent oil changes can wear out the drain-plug threads over time7475767778798081828384.

    Choosing viscosity by listening to the engine, not just mileage. A separate framework for the 5W-30-to-10W40 question: stick with synthetic (5W-30 or 5W-40) oil as long as the engine's running sound stays consistent with baseline — i.e. no audible change after an oil change. Only once engine noise noticeably increases following an oil change should a switch to semi-synthetic be considered, since that noise increase signals seal degradation and rising oil consumption consistent with a more worn engine. The recommendation is to base the decision on how the engine actually sounds/behaves rather than on a fixed mileage milestone. Consensus in this thread: stick with 5W-30 or 5W-40 — do not jump directly from 5W-30 to 10W-40 as sometimes claimed online. The "switch to 10W-40 after 100,000+ km" claim circulating online is disputed: some argue it's fine at high mileage, others warn it risks clogging the engine's narrow oil channels (масляные каналы узкие)8586878889909192. Conflict: this directly parallels the existing 10W-40 caution already on this card — both sources agree 10W-40 carries real risk on this engine and should not be adopted casually.

    Grade/API summary and counterfeit-avoidance strategy. Summarizing and reinforcing what's already on this card: any synthetic 5W-30 or 5W-40 meeting API SN or SP is acceptable (SL/SM also work but are outdated ratings). To avoid counterfeits: buy only from official/verified sellers, or prefer oil sold in metal canisters (more expensive to counterfeit than plastic). Recommended lower-counterfeit-risk brands in the Russian market: Lukoil, Syntec, Mirax, Gazpromneft (domestic brands, less frequently counterfeited). By contrast, premium international brands (Motul, Total, Elf, Mobil) reportedly see roughly 10x more counterfeiting in this market — buy these with extra caution. Kixx synthetic is considered premium but rarely counterfeited due to relative obscurity; it was previously sold in purple packaging and is now mainly available via online marketplaces93949596979899100101102103104105106107108109110.

    Standards restated concisely — SAE, API, ACEA, and a Mazda-3 caution. Recommended viscosity grades: SAE 5W-30 or 5W-40. API standards: SL, SN, or SP. ACEA standards: A3/B4 or A5/B5. Owners are cautioned not to use oils formulated/rated specifically for the Mazda 3 or other vehicles with a different spec, despite the shared Duratec HE engine lineage. Switching to a properly-specified synthetic oil (e.g. Liqui Moly MOLYGEN) is reported to noticeably improve engine noise, throttle responsiveness, and overall smoothness of operation111112113114115.

    Liqui Moly MOLYGEN NEW GENERATION 5W-30 — real-world results. One owner installed Liqui Moly MOLYGEN NEW GENERATION 5W-30 synthetic oil (5L bottles sourced from a verified Ozon seller) in May 2026. Reported benefits: a noticeably quieter engine, reduced engine noise/whistle, and smoother overall operation. The recommendation originally came from a Mazda 6 owner but was verified as compatible with the X80 given the shared engine platform; the seller guarantees authenticity116117118119120121.

    Viscosity choice restated — Mazda-derived engine, manual minimum vs. practice. The X80's Mazda-derived engine has a manual-specified minimum SM grade; one experienced owner runs SL grade instead. If budget allows, a C3-rated viscosity is recommended. The manual does not state a specific upper viscosity limit — only the minimum detergency/quality standard122123.

    API class progression — SM (outdated) → SN → SP, restated across two independent, earlier threads. Two separate, earlier community discussions independently converge on the same guidance already documented on this card: SM is the manual's minimum/outdated baseline; SN is the common current standard; SP is the newest and best. SM-SN are backward compatible with each other; SP is superior to both. Both threads agree that oil quality and brand reliability matter more than chasing the newest classification letter alone, and that timely oil changes matter more than the exact classification chosen124125126127.

    Russian VMP-brand oil — positive owner experience, cross-vehicle use. Independent of the counterfeit-avoidance and premium-brand guidance already on this card, the domestic ВМП (VMP) brand — which specializes in engine/gearbox additives — received positive feedback from owners. One owner filled twice with VMP and rated its quality as equivalent to imported oils; another owner uses the same brand on a second vehicle (a Hyundai Kreta), reporting a good additive package for engine longevity per community consensus128129130131.

    Brand/viscosity recommendations — restated with concrete change-interval and consumption data. An earlier, independent set of brand recommendations for the X80: VMP Avto (5W-30 or 5W-40 — no oil consumption reported at 120,000 km after two full changes), Lukoil Genesis (the factory fill — no issues reported, available in 5W-40 or 5W-30), and Gazpromneft Premium N 5W-40. ZIC 5W-40 is flagged as one to avoid — reported to increase engine noise and oil consumption. Recommended change interval: 7,000 km. Seasonal viscosity guidance: 5W-30 in winter, 5W-40 in summer. Idemitsu and Shell as listed in the owner's manual are described as recommendations only rather than a strict requirement; the factory-filled Lukoil Genesis is noted as appropriate specifically for Kaliningrad-assembled cars132133134135136137138139140141142143144145146.

    5W-40 synthetic, API SN/CF, brand-agnostic — from the second Telegram group. A separate community (FAW BESTURN X80 group) restates the core recommendation independently: 5W-40 synthetic, meeting API SN/CF, is recommended as long as it's genuine (non-counterfeit) — the responder notes brand choice is largely a matter of preference ("depends on your faith") among reputable options like Lukoil, Gazprom, Syntek, and Shell147.

    All-weather 5W-40 recommended for hot climates — from the second Telegram group. When asked specifically which oil is best for hot weather, a separate response from the same second group states 5W-40 is the ideal viscosity for the X80 in any weather, including hot climates — directly relevant to owners in warm-climate markets like Syria148149.

    Engine oil capacity restated — 4.3-4.4L actual vs. 2L on paper, from the second Telegram group. A separate thread in the second group repeats a capacity discrepancy already documented on this card: the spec sheet states 2 liters, but the actual full capacity is ~4.3-4.5 liters. Multiple owners confirm filling to the upper dipstick mark for 4.3-4.4L with no issues over several years of ownership — consistent with the 4.3L (with filter) figure already on this card150151152153154155.

    Where the community disagrees

    • Multiple threads on this card disagree about whether 10W-40 is safe on the X80's engine at higher mileage — some recommend it past 150,000 km, others (including a separate exchange on this same card) warn it risks clogging the engine's narrow oil channels; treat 10W-40 adoption as debated rather than settled.
