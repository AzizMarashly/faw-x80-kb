    Spark plugs — counterfeits and authenticity

    Tags: #spark-plugs · #torch

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Part of the spark-plug series — overview: Spark plugs — options, prices, reliability.

    Counterfeit / reliability warning — real-world failure case. One owner bought TORCH QH6RIU11 iridium and AMD AMD.PL116 plugs from Ozon and AliExpress; all failed (burned out) within 1,000 km12. The resulting rich fuel mixture caused hard starting (required clutch-assisted starts, which the owner notes risked damaging the transmission) and led to a cascade of paid diagnostics: 4,000 RUB for a diagnostic scan, 7,000 RUB for a valve clearance adjustment, and 3,000 RUB for an EGR cleaning (RU market, November 2024)34. Switching to spark plugs bought from a local/official shop resolved the problem completely56. Community takeaway: prefer buying from an official dealership over online marketplaces, since defective counterfeit batches of Chinese-made plugs are a recurring issue online78.

    NGK 3811 counterfeits — burned electrodes at 15,000 km. Counterfeit NGK 3811 plugs are flagged as a specific risk: one owner's set had burned electrodes with a 2.5mm gap after only 15,000 km. Cheap/counterfeit plugs in general are linked to poor fuel economy and, in the worst case, piston/valve burnout and shortened engine life; genuine plugs should show a proper solder/metal overlay (напайка) on the electrode. Denso TV16TT was reported as a reliable alternative after this NGK 3811 failure9101112131415.

    Genuine vs. counterfeit — burned electrodes despite a correct-looking plug. One owner's plug set (make debated in-thread as Denso or NGK) showed a burned electrode after a video/photo comparison was shared161718. A separate owner reports a set that visually looked correct — proper braze, even electrodes, matching gaps — but burned out after only 14,000 km, underscoring that counterfeits can be visually hard to distinguish from genuine plugs19. Another member notes that calorific (heat) number mismatches specifically cause incorrect operation if the wrong plug is selected, independent of counterfeiting20.

    Extended electrode-type discussion — RTIP vs. RIU, iridium core vs. platinum pad, and OEM-supplier uncertainty. A long thread examines close-up photos of installed plugs to determine authenticity and type. Consensus points: (1) RTIP-suffix plugs have an iridium core plus a platinum braze/pad on the ground electrode; RIU-suffix plugs have an iridium core without the platinum pad, and use a U-shaped side electrode where a braze pad would not normally be visible anyway — so "no visible braze" is not itself proof of a fake if the plug is a RIU-type212223. (2) One owner's photographed "genuine" reference plug does show both iridium core and platinum pad with a 1.1mm gap and full part markings2425. (3) Several long-time members state they have never actually seen genuine factory RTIP plugs sold at official dealers — dealers reportedly fit the non-braze analog instead, casting some doubt on how common true OEM RTIP units are in the retail supply chain, though this is recollection rather than documented proof2627. (4) General sentiment: modern iridium-core plugs "these days" are broadly similar in quality regardless of visible braze, and the factory manual does not prohibit using non-platinum-pad plugs282930.

    Counterfeit AliExpress plugs caused misfiring — verify authenticity before installing. A cautionary report: one owner (Сергей) ordered Torch QH6RTIP-13 plugs from an "official store" listing on AliExpress; one of the plugs arrived defective, causing engine misfiring and triggering error codes, and he had to revert to his original plugs. Lesson: verify plug authenticity carefully before installation, consistent with the broader counterfeit/authenticity concerns already documented on this card31.
