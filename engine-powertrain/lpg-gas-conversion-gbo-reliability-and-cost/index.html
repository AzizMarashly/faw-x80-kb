    LPG (GBO) conversion — reliability and cost

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    Long-term reliability — 85,000 km on gas. One owner (Eduard) installed an LPG (GBO — газобаллонное оборудование) system and has run 85,000 km on gas fuel with the system reported as reliable; he is available for consultation within the community12.

    Cost-benefit — only pays off over a long ownership horizon. LPG conversion is only economical if the owner plans to keep the vehicle 10+ years; the payback period typically requires that timeframe given the high upfront installation cost12.

    Effect on engine — cleaner burn, higher octane, slightly higher combustion temperature. LPG burns cleaner than petrol, producing less carbon buildup on pistons and valves, and — unlike petrol — does not wash oil film off the cylinder walls. LPG's higher octane rating (~105 vs. 92-95 for petrol) helps prevent detonation and reduces shock loads on engine parts. However, gas combustion temperature runs 4-5°C higher than petrol, slightly raising engine operating temperature. One owner reported the engine's compression ratio increased from 10-11 to 12.6 after 40,000 km on a properly tuned gas system (total mileage at time of report: 158,000 km)34567. Conflict: a mechanic with 10 years' experience (Валерий) argues gas reduces engine life and stresses the cylinder head/gasket (ГБЦ); another owner (Игорь) argues proper ECU tuning minimizes this risk, citing gas's cleaner burn, lack of carbon buildup, and higher octane as factors that can increase engine life — reflecting an ongoing, unresolved debate in the wider Russian automotive community.

    Economics and mandatory maintenance requirement (2026 pricing). Fuel cost comparison: 30L of petrol costs roughly 1,700 RUB, the same volume of LPG roughly 700 RUB (RU market, Feb 2026) — a significant per-fill saving that reinforces the long-horizon payback case already documented on this card. Critical maintenance requirement specific to gas conversion: valve clearance must be checked every 20,000-30,000 km — this is mandatory, not optional, because the valve gap increases faster on gas over time and must be adjusted or the valves will burn. This requires removing the valve cover and replacing shims (see the shim-adjustment card for the general procedure) — it needs real mechanical skill. Because the X80 has no hydraulic lifters (mechanical only, consistent with the valve-lifter card elsewhere in this section), skipping this check becomes expensive and time-consuming to recover from. One owner reports 185,000 km on gas without issues, attributed to careful, non-aggressive driving (a family member drives it gently). Not recommended for aggressive drivers8910111213141516171819202122232425262728293031. Conflict: consistent with the longevity dispute already documented on this card — some users report gas conversion works fine over high mileage (Фанис, 185,000 km), others (Фаб) warn against it specifically because of the lack of hydraulic lifters.

    Long-term ownership report — 42,000 RUB unregistered install, ~150,000 RUB/year savings. One owner (Arсhi 77) installed LPG at 42,000 RUB (RU market, April 2026) (unregistered install), 4th/5th-generation propane equipment, on a car doing 30-35k km/year. Fuel cost comparison: propane 19.90 RUB/liter (RU market, April 2026) vs. 70+ RUB/liter for 95-octane petrol, yielding an estimated ~150,000 RUB/year (RU market, April 2026) savings. Maintenance cost: 1,400 RUB every 10-15k km (RU market, April 2026). After 160,000 km on propane, the engine reportedly stays clean with no carbon buildup. The system automatically switches to petrol at high RPM to protect the valves. The X80 has no valve-gap compensators, but this owner reports successful long-term use regardless; notes valve gaps still need annual monitoring. The spare tire was removed to fit the 55L toroidal propane tank in the wheel well; dual-tank range is 900-1,000 km. Many taxi/commercial drivers in the Dagestan region reportedly run the same setup long-term32333435363738394041424344454647.

    Second long-term report — 70,000 km over 2 years, ~30% fuel-cost reduction, ECU tuning debate. A separate owner reports ~70,000 km over 2 years on LPG with no problems, claiming roughly 30% fuel-cost savings at 100+ RUB/liter petrol prices (June 2026). This owner notes that professional LPG installers familiar with the platform can tune the system electronically via computer, and pushes back on the concern that improper tuning damages valves — modern tuning is done electronically by a specialist service rather than manual adjustment48495051.

    LPG system setup details and valve-clearance debate (July 2026). Equipment cost had roughly doubled by July 2026 due to a shortage. Typical setup: 55L toroidal balloon under the spare wheel, BRC or equivalent controller. Reported claim: valve-clearance regulation is not required during installation if the system is correctly tuned, because the ECU compensates for thermal clearance at high RPM (e.g. fuel trim adjusted at 4,000 RPM to hit a stoichiometric mix). Nonetheless, checking clearances every 30,000 km is recommended, adjusting if needed (optimal exhaust-valve clearance: 0.35mm). One owner reports 60,000 km on gas with 10-15% higher fuel consumption than petrol but roughly 300,000 RUB (RU market, July 2026) saved over 2 years vs. petrol525354555657585960616263. Conflict: fuel-mixing compensation is disputed — Victor credits ECU tuning for handling thermal valve clearance automatically; Фаб emphasizes the risk of skipping valve-clearance regulation given the X80's lack of hydraulic compensators; Arсhi 77 counters that proper professional installation and high-RPM ECU tuning handle this adequately, citing 60,000 km of trouble-free use as evidence.

    Valve and thermal design considerations for LPG — restated. The X80 uses direct mechanical valve clearance with no hydraulic compensators and no built-in auto-adjustment. Safe LPG operation is reported to require: (1) ECU fuel trim at high RPM (~4,000 RPM) to reduce lean-burn tendency, (2) periodic manual clearance checks (every 30k km), (3) professional (not curbside/DIY) installation. Both methane (CNG) and propane (LPG) are viable fuel choices; methane doesn't require a spare-wheel-well tank but is rarer to find. Propane availability is tied to domestic refinery (NPZ) output rather than being independently supplied546465666768.

    Comparison anecdote — 200,000 km on a different vehicle (Hover H3), shared as LPG reliability evidence. Not an X80 report, but shared in the same thread as relevant experience: one member (Niko75) ran an official BRC LPG installation (toroidal tank under the spare wheel) on a Great Wall Hover H3, installed in 2018 for 32,000 RUB (RU market, 2018), and drove 200,000 km before selling the car in 2023 with no valve burn-out or major issues, on a 30,000 km valve-adjustment interval plus periodic GBO service. Another member (Василий Егоров) noted that official installers/dealers in his region tend to drag their feet on scheduling the mandatory valve-clearance regulation service6369.

    Tuning an LPG system around valve clearance, and installer quality (Oct 2026). Victor 🇷🇺 says that even where valve clearances cannot be adjusted (no hydraulic lifters), the LPG system (ГБО) can be tuned to switch to petrol injection above 4,000 rpm 70. Arсhi 77, who has an LPG installation, recalls an earlier discussion in which someone argued that gas bends valves and that gas is bad for this engine, and says theory does not always mean practice; he advises nothing, "do as prescribed", and remembers that installers are often bad 7172. Victor 🇷🇺 states what he actually wrote (the 4,000 rpm petrol switch above) rather than that LPG is harmful 70.

    Does LPG still pay off? (Oct 2026). Victor 🇷🇺 drives about 14,000 km a year and says the running-cost gap on his X80 is too small to justify an installation, though he dislikes the car's consumption 73. His brother's Murano runs on LPG, but with LPG at 54 RUB (RU market, Oct 2026; unit not stated) a Peugeot is now cheaper on petrol 74. Arсhi 77 installed LPG when it cost 25 and petrol 60 (an earlier, undated period, as recalled in Oct 2026) and agrees it is not worth installing now, except in Dagestan, where he says LPG is 55-60 against petrol at 120 (as claimed in Oct 2026) 75. Victor 🇷🇺 doubts the 120 figure: in 2024 petrol there cost the same as elsewhere 76.

    Where the community disagrees

    • Community disagreement on LPG's effect on engine longevity: a mechanic with 10 years' experience (Валерий) argues gas use reduces engine life and stresses the cylinder head/gasket; another owner (Игорь) argues proper ECU tuning minimizes this risk and that gas's cleaner burn and higher octane can actually extend engine life. Not resolved within this data batch — reflects an ongoing debate in the wider Russian automotive community.
    • Some users report LPG conversion works fine over high mileage without issues (Фанис, 185,000 km on gas, careful driving); others (Фаб) warn against gas conversion specifically because the X80 has no hydraulic lifters, making the mandatory 20,000-30,000 km valve-clearance check both critical and easy to neglect. Not resolved within this data batch.
    • Fuel-mixing/valve-clearance compensation on LPG is disputed: Victor credits ECU tuning at high RPM with handling thermal valve clearance automatically; Фаб emphasizes the valve-clearance regulation risk given the X80's lack of hydraulic compensators; Arсhi 77 counters that proper professional installation and ECU tuning at high RPM handle this adequately, citing 60,000 km of trouble-free use as evidence. Consistent with, and reinforces, the earlier longevity/hydraulic-lifters conflicts already documented on this card.
