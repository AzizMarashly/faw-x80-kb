    Lower arm bushings — pressing vs arm swap

    Tags: #control-arm · #bushings · #diy

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Bushing-removal tool tip: for pressing sailentbloks (bushings) in and out, an impact perforator (industrial power drill fitted with a special tubular pusher, 20-100mm range) is reported as faster and more repeatable than the traditional manual-puller-plus-vise method. The tubular pusher set can be fabricated from standard pipe for DIY use 1.

    Rear longitudinal-arm bushing replacement — DIY video guide: replacing the rear longitudinal control-arm bushing (see the SBX80PRODZK bushing on the rear bushing numbers card) is achievable DIY using heat plus a press. A YouTube guide (https://youtu.be/O3UCqnCH38M) documents the procedure; it requires a heat source, manual tools, and careful technique. For comparison, a commercial garage with a lift and hydraulic press completes the same job in roughly 15 minutes — DIY takes considerably longer 2.

    Sealant-block (bushing) DIY press-out/press-in methods: bushings can be replaced without removing the entire control arm, using any of: (1) a heat-plus-drift method — heat the bushing, drive it out with a drift, then press the new one in; (2) a pneumatic hammer/perforator to assist pressing; (3) an improvised press made from a long bolt, stacked washers, and a wrench; (4) a rotary tool with a chisel to mill the old bushing out. Removing the full arm and using a shop press is the cleanest method, but all of the above DIY approaches are reported to work; if the ball joint is still serviceable, replacing just the bushings and keeping it is a valid option 3.

    Rear longitudinal arm bushing — press-only, no simple swap (hardest part of a full suspension refresh): one owner replacing the rear longitudinal arm bushing alongside the front transverse arms and a lower right arm found every other part straightforward, but the rear longitudinal bushing "press-fit only" design made it far more difficult — no standard puller could reach it, with obstructions on all sides. The owner concludes they would rather pay a shop for this specific job next time. With two people working together it still took roughly 4 hours 4567.

    Rear lower control-arm bushings — individually replaceable, retainer-ring type preferred, cost comparison (Nov 2025): confirming and extending the SBX80PRODZK bushing already documented on the rear bushing numbers card, the lower rear control arm's bushing (сайлентблок) can be replaced individually without swapping the whole arm 89. Buy only the bushing with a metal retainer ring, not the plain-rubber type — the ring type is harder to press in but performs better and is "the best of the silentblocks available" 1011. Mazda 6 GG-sourced bushings (and complete Mazda 6 GG arm assemblies) both fit the X80's rear 12131415. Cost comparison: a full rear control-arm assembly runs roughly 7,500–8,000 RUB (RU market, November 2025), versus individually replacing just the bushings, which one owner found considerably cheaper and did themselves 16.

    Lower control arm bushings — Mazda 6 GG mixed-parts replacement: lower control arm bushings can be replaced using components from Mazda 6 GG (Mazda Atenza GG generation), typically as a mixed build — some factory-original components combined with aftermarket-compatible parts. New adjustment bolts are usually needed (2 pieces, for camber/toe adjustment) 17181920.

    Oct 2026 update — removing the front lower arm with a puller. Сергей Елисеев says a puller (съёмник) makes lower-arm removal easy: tighten the puller, jack up the arm, and the joint releases with a click; raising two wheels simplifies the whole procedure 21. Victor could not remove the factory bushing without one, says a puller is definitely needed, and notes that his own puller is only for steering joints — arms need a heavier tool 22.
