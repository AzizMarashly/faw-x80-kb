    Front coil springs — failure and sourcing

    Tags: #springs · #price · #mazda-6-compat · #conflict

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Community members disagree on this topic — both positions are reported.

    Front coil springs on the X80 are a known failure point, and the parts market is split between scarce genuine FAW springs, aftermarket analogs and Mazda 6 cross-fits. This card holds the core failure and price facts; the details are split into the sibling cards listed at the end.

    Failure pattern: front springs can break as early as ~45,000 km, and multiple later reports confirm ongoing failures well past that (one owner's left and right springs both failed by 196,000 km combined mileage) 12. Where the spring breaks matters for safety: a break at the coil ends still allows some further driving, but a break in the middle section calls for immediate replacement 2. A separate report describes a right front spring failing with a loud metallic sound audible inside the cabin — the vehicle may show only slight settling, not obviously visible without close inspection, and a spring can fail even while the car is parked 3. Fatigue cracking at the mid-coil is the reported failure mode in another case 4. When replacing, both front springs are typically done together 2.

    When replacing shocks, also replace the springs — this is standard practice reported by the community when doing front suspension work 1.

    Front vs rear parts availability (disputed): sources indicate that if a rear shock leaks, only OEM parts are available with no cross-brand alternative, while front analogs from other brands are more commonly available — but the thread does not fully resolve whether this rule applies equally to both axles 1 (conflict, see below).

    Pricing: OEM front springs cost 11,000–12,000 RUB per unit (RU market, mid/late 2024) 5; a later report cites ~5,000 RUB each (RU market, November 2024) from an official dealer for the fatigue-crack repair 4; a January 2025 dealer purchase of one front (right) spring cost 3,000 RUB (RU market, January 2025), with a free car wash included 6; Kitparts sold a full front spring set for ~3,000 RUB in January 2025 (RU market), noting slightly different pricing/coils between manual (MKPP) and automatic (AKPP) transmission variants without detailing the exact spec difference 7.

    Aftermarket alternative: part number 5CA1340011, sold by KITZAP, at ~2,100 RUB (RU market, September 2024) — reported as lasting 30,000–50,000 km versus the OEM spring, which is harder to find used and generally outlasts the analog 5. As a workaround for repeated failures, some owners use spacers (prostavki) to reduce spring compression and extend service life, at the cost of some ride comfort and possible suspension-geometry effects 4.

    Disagreement — do Mazda 6 springs cross-fit? Some owners report that Mazda 6 springs (per the Exist parts catalog) may fit. Sergey Arapkin explicitly disputes this, stating the X80's springs are thicker and use different fork/mounting connectors than Mazda 6's, so they do not fit. The community's practical recommendation is to source from an official FAW dealer or specialized retailers (KitParts, Exist), and to buy a complete strut assembly rather than a bare spring where possible 89.
