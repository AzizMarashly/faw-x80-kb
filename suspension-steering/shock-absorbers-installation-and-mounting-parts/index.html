    Shock installation, bump stops and mounts

    Tags: #shock-absorbers · #installation

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Replacing shocks at home, spring compressors, bump stops (отбойники), dust boots, strut support bearings, mounting bolts and other installation details. Overview: Shock absorbers — overview and platform baseline. See also shock absorber replacement and front strut dust boots sourcing.

    Bump stops (отбойники) reduce knocking: the X80 uses soft dampers, with much of the spring load concentrated on the rubber bump stops rather than the shocks themselves. Rear knocking/clunking noise while driving was resolved by replacing the rear bump stops, with a bonus softer ride feel afterward. Front bump stops are recommended for replacement on the same schedule for consistent comfort 1. A separate report confirms the same fix: a dull, low-speed metallic clunk traced to worn shock-absorber bump stops was eliminated by replacing the rear units 2.

    Front shock dust boot sourcing: front shock-absorber dust boots (пыльники) are available via Kitparts and Ozon catalogs. An attempted substitute — Lynx-brand boots intended for a Mazda CX-5 — did not fit (the seals were too short). A working alternative: a Lynx-brand set of two dust boots plus two bump stops (отбойники), sourced through KitZap with delivery 3.

    Front strut support bearing (опорный подшипник) — pricing and sourcing: the front strut top support bearing is sold as two separate components — the housing/cup (чашка) and the bearing itself (подшипник). Factory pricing: cup 2,000 RUB (RU market, July 2025), bearing 1,200 RUB (3,200 RUB combined). One shop's retail price for the cup alone was 1,060 RUB (RU market, July 2025). Sourcing options: kitzapchasti.ru (sometimes doesn't list the bearing as a separate SKU), kitparts.ru (order-only, ~4-day wait), and zzap.ru (a parts aggregator that shows multiple store options by part number). Some retailers list only the cup, without the bearing as a separate purchasable item 4.

    DIY rear shock replacement — needs a lift/pit, spring compressor essential (Sept 2025): rear shock replacement is not practical at home without a vehicle lift or service pit; even with an impact wrench and a helper, budget 2+ hours per side 567. The spring must be compressed for removal/installation — one owner's first pair of compressors failed (snapped) on the very first strut and again while releasing the last one, while a second, sturdier compressor set held up 8910. A recommended source: a 280mm double-hook spring compressor set (2 pcs) from Ozon, well lubricated before use; several owners report it holding up well, though a poor-quality compressor can snap and cause injury (numbed hands reported after one such failure) 11121314.

    Front strut top mount ("опора") — also sourceable from FAW B50 (Feb 2026): a brief confirmation, separate from the SAM-4101/GJ6E strut mount bearing prices and sourcing in the prices and costs card — the front strut top mount itself can be sourced from the FAW B50 as a cross-fit part 15.

    KYB shocks — longer stroke makes spring work much easier: KYB aftermarket shocks have a significantly longer stroke than the factory X80 units, so compressing the spring for install/removal is far easier and does not require a spring-compressor press — the stock shocks' very short stroke is what makes that job hard 1617.

    Replacement front shock rod stub too long (38mm vs 35mm spec) — machine-shop fix: when replacing front shocks at high mileage (100,000+ km), a new unit can arrive with a longer rod stub than spec (38mm measured vs the correct ~35mm), throwing off suspension geometry and causing rattling/rubbing. Diagnosis: measure the rod stub length against the original (or with a ruler if the original isn't available). Fix: a machine shop shortens the stub by cutting ~2cm off, then the strut is reassembled. One owner discovered this at 130,000 km, after already replacing two front hub bearings, during a suspension service — always verify both old and new shock condition/dimensions before installing 1819202122.

    Rear shock mounting bolts seized in rubber sleeves at 62,000 km: at 62,000 km, rear shock mounting bolts had corroded/bonded (galvanic action between the rubber sleeve and metal bolt) so badly they had to be cut out with an angle grinder. Prevention for future installs: coat the bolt with graphite lubricant or Lithol-type grease before assembly 232425.

    Rear shock dust boots — Mazda 6 KYB kit, cheaper than some local sources: dust boots and rebound bump stops (пыльники + отбойники) for rear shocks are available as a Mazda 6 GG-fit KYB 910 107 kit (Mazda 6 '02+ fitment); found cheaper on Ozon than some alternative sources; one installer reports successful use 2627.

    Factory assembly hooks on the shocks — safe to remove, plus a cheap angle-adjustment tool: stock X80 shocks have small hooks/brackets that look like manufacturing/assembly aids (likely used to hang the strut on the line before final bolting) — they serve no function after installation and tend to catch on things during removal/installation, especially off a lift. Many owners recommend simply removing them. A cheap (~100–1,000₽) fuse-box-style tool can help set the strut angle correctly during installation 28293031.

    Metallic clank after rear shock replacement — likely bushings, not a loose fastener: if a metallic knocking sound appears specifically after replacing the rear shock absorbers, and a visual re-check finds everything properly torqued, the noise is more likely coming from worn suspension bushings (сайленты) than an actually loose fastener — inspect the bushings rather than continuing to re-check the shock mounting hardware 3233.
