    Rear wheel hub bearing

    Tags: #mazda-6-compat · #part-number · #wheel-hub · #bearing · #price · #rear-axle

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    The X80 rear wheel hub is a press-fit hub-and-bearing assembly (part FC012615X / FA012615XB) that is shared with the Mazda 6 GG and the FAW B50, so cross-brand and used units are common. The front hub is a different, bolt-on design — see the front wheel hub bearing card.

    Part numbers and what is sold

    Part number: FC012615X, the rear wheel bearing/hub assembly.12 A rear hub assembly listed under FA012615XB (marked "NEW" on the marketplace listing, sold on Ozon) appears to be a labeling variant of the same part rather than a different one; treat FC012615X and FA012615XB as the same assembly until a discrepancy is confirmed.345 A later, independent report (March 2026) reconfirmed FA012615XB as the complete rear hub assembly, available on OZON.67

    It is sold only as a complete sealed assembly — individual bearings cannot be serviced, reamed or replaced separately.28 A follow-up question about a standalone rear-right bearing part number was answered directly: the rear hub bearing is not sold separately and must be replaced as the full assembly (FA012615XB, "NEW" on marketplace listings), available on Ozon and similar retailers.91011 The assembly is also available from Wildberries and Avito.1

    Design and serviceability

    The rear hub is press-fitted onto the rear axle spindle with a single retaining bolt, and no easy Chinese-brand analog exists yet; Krauf appears as a generic option. (The front hub bolts directly to the steering knuckle — a different design with more limited aftermarket options.)121314151617

    Reports disagree on whether the rear unit may be opened:

    • One report says not to disassemble and re-grease the bearing: it develops wear grooves and fails prematurely, and a replacement hub assembly is reported as cheaper than a quality bearing alone anyway.8
    • Another says that, unlike the front hub, the rear hub is disassemblable.1819
    • When installing a swapped or used hub, one 2025-11 report advises to fully disassemble it and repack it with high-quality grease, since original hubs need proper greasing to avoid premature wear.2021222324 The used Mazda 6 hub in a separate case also "required minor disassembly and re-lubrication" before it fitted and worked reliably.25
    • A further report contrasts the X80 with the B50, which has a partially serviceable hub: the X80 rear hub is described as a sealed, non-disassemblable unit.2627282930313233343536373839404142

    Symptoms and service life

    The bearing typically develops play around 70,000–100,000 km with an audible grinding noise (гул). The noise can persist for an extended distance (7,000+ km) before outright failure, but prompt replacement is recommended to avoid secondary damage to other drivetrain components (front CV joints, potential rear-axle effects).2

    A case from Dec 2025 describes a growling/turbo-like sound starting around 60 km/h and increasing with acceleration, with no steering-wheel vibration; on inspection the rear hub bearing had disintegrated. The community treats this as a known X80 failure mode consistent with the 70,000–100,000 km pattern.434445

    Reported service life varies widely. Owners report the rear hub bearing commonly wears out around 256,000 km.464748 One rear replacement was documented at 130,000 km total mileage (the owner had already replaced both front bearings).495051525354 Conflict: the rear hubs on the X80 reportedly fail prematurely for some owners even with regular tire rotation and routine maintenance — one owner's left rear hub failed repeatedly before reaching 30,000 km each time, replacement costing ~2,500₽ per hub — while a second owner reports the rear hubs still original at 167,000 km. No consensus root cause emerged; possibly a defective batch or differences in driving/maintenance pattern.2627282930313233343536373839404142

    Cross-fit and alternatives

    Mazda 6. The same hub/bearing assembly is used on the Mazda 6 GG (2002–2008 in one report, 2003–2007 generation in another), so a used Mazda 6 rear hub can be substituted.251819 The rear hub of the Mazda 6 MPV GG/LW also fits the FAW X80 (2025-11 report) — unlike the front hub, which is not swappable.3452021222324 Rear wheel bearings sourced from the Mazda 6 may fit; Ildgin (a Korean Mazda-parts supplier) bearings have been used successfully for a rear wheel bearing replacement.55565753 When buying a Mazda 6-sourced used unit or a no-name aftermarket assembly, confirm exact compatibility before installing — some no-name analogs are reported as equivalent in quality to branded versions.158

    FAW B50. The rear wheel hub (ступица) is reported as identical between the X80 and the FAW B50 (Feb 2026), so it can be sourced from B50 parts catalogs — often at a lower cost than X80-specific listings.5960

    Used donor units. One owner sourced a used Mazda 6 hub via Avito for ~1,000 RUB (RU market, September 2024) including delivery; it required minor disassembly and re-lubrication but fitted and worked reliably.25 Another fitted a used Mazda 6GG rear hub from Avito for 910 RUB, which installed "like original" and carries Japanese markings. Caution: out of curiosity about a humming noise, the owner later disassembled it and found deep indentation marks of unknown origin on the inner race — inspect a used donor's races carefully before trusting it long-term; its longevity was not yet established.61

    Selecting a unit (Aug 2026). An owner choosing between two listings — a Mazda 6 rear hub assembly SAT ST-G14V-26-15X at 2,240 RUB (RU market, August 2026) (Irkutsk, 2026-08-20) and a Tatsumi-brand Mazda 6/CX-7 (2007+) rear hub TDE5062 at 7,430 RUB (discounted from 7,660 RUB, same seller/date) — was advised to buy the hub with the ABS sensor connector already attached (a plain non-ABS hub will not work on ABS-equipped cars) and specifically the left-side hub, not the right, since front and rear hubs on this platform are handed and the two sides are not interchangeable.6263646566 This conflicts with the front card, which reports front hubs as identical on both sides; the left/right remark may apply to the rear hub only, and no one reconciled the two.

    Other options and prices.

    • Stellox part 43-29349-SX (wheel hub bearing kit): a marketplace listing sold it as "ORIGINAL" for 14,924 RUB (2025).1819
    • OEM FAW replacement via chinaspare.ru for approximately 4,000 RUB (as of 2025-03-28). Budget cross-brand alternatives beyond Mazda are also reported to work if fitment (tooth count on the ABS ring, physical dimensions) is verified first — sources include KitZap, KitParts and Chinaspare.464748
    • ILJIN IJ122042 (Mazda-based) at ~5,450₽ via Emex (2026), or the lower-cost B-ring BK3913 at ~2,500₽.121314151617 A real install of the ILJIN unit cost ~5,450₽ in parts plus ~4,000₽ labor, and the lower hub-mounting bolts came out easily without needing to be cut or drilled.67
    • Adapting or customizing non-matching aftermarket hub parts is discouraged: one such attempt (~1,000₽) still ended in failure. Stick to the complete, correctly matched assembly (FC012615X / FA012615XB).6869

    Durability by brand — mixed results. B-ring BK3913 bearings on a FAW B50 (the X80's platform mate) show good durability in two reported cases: one unit at 200,000+ km with no noise, a second at 15,000 km with no issues yet — though one commenter (Victor) is skeptical of B-ring quality overall. Pricing recap: ILJIN rear hub ~5,450₽ vs. B-ring ~2,500₽. Cheaper bearings can fail early (an Astra-brand example: noise after only 7,000 km) while expensive brands can also fail unpredictably (a Koyo example: failure at just 2,000 km on a B50). Takeaway: quality is inconsistent across brands regardless of price; waterproofing grease during installation is reported to improve longevity.70717273747576

    Replacement procedure

    Quick method (no special tools, about 1 hour at most). Remove the caliper, insert a bolt into a hole in the brake disc to stop it rotating while breaking the hub nut loose (36 mm socket, with leverage), then pull the hub off by hand — it comes free easily. Install the new hub, torque the nut and peen (stake) the nut collar to lock it. No puller or other special tool is required. Reassemble the caliper and wheel and drive.77787980

    Other accounts. A 2025-04 report describes the same job as removing the brake caliper (суппорт) and unbolting the hub, with no difference between the left and right sides.818283 A detailed 2026-05 walkthrough corrects the socket size: use a 36 mm socket, not 32 mm, which is easy to assume incorrectly. Remove the caliper together with its mounting bracket (keep the two assembled), leave the brake disc in place (it is held on by friction alone; no bolts secure it to the hub), then replace the bearing. Clean the brake pads after removal with a dedicated pad-spreader tool. Hub nut torque is 240–270 Nm, achievable with a battery-powered impact driver. ILDJIN bearings (a Korean Mazda OEM supplier) were confirmed to work well and are endorsed by the community as a good-quality choice.495051525354

    Cross-reference and swap notes. The rear hub assembly is listed for the X80 under FC012615X; a member says it also appears for the B50 under G14V2615XB or FA012615XB and looks identical, and a photographed YVA box is labelled FAW B50 FC012615X.848586 A complete rear hub for the FAW Besturn X80 NEW is listed under FA012615XB on Ozon (October 2025).87 One owner's rear hub swap took about one hour; the axle nut needed a 36 mm socket and a breaker pipe, which he had to borrow from neighbours.88
