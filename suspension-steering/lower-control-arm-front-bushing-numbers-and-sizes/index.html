    Front lower arm bushings — numbers and sizes

    Tags: #control-arm · #bushings · #part-number · #mazda-6-compat · #silent-block

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Front lower ("longitudinal"/curved) control arm: FAW part number FC0134J00, sold only as a complete assembled unit — individual rubber bushings cannot be purchased separately from OEM suppliers. Bushings can be manually pressed out and replaced with aftermarket units, but sourcing loose bushings requires matching the exact dimensions 1. Aftermarket bushings are available via Chinaspare.ru (chinaspare.ru/c/saylentblok-perednego-nizhnego-krivogo-rychaga-faw), with catalog part numbers 61-04503-3X and 61-04504-3X, cross-checked against FAW and other Asian-manufacturer diagrams 2. Bushings for the front lower arm are also confirmed available for the facelifted (restyled) X80 via Kitzap.ru; the rear suspension uses similar bushings, and owners have successfully replaced the front set with rear work planned separately 3.

    Cross-fit — Mazda 6 GG lower arm: FAW X80 lower control arms cross-reference to Mazda 6 (2002-2007 GG generation) and Mazda Atenza. Part number GJ6A34300 GG. Market price ~3,200 RUB (RU market, October 2024), available on M-Avto and other Russian automotive marketplaces 4.

    Front control arms — detailed Mazda 6 GG compatibility: both the lower and upper front control arms are sourced from the Mazda 6 GG (first generation); measurements and silentblock dimensions match the Mazda GG parts per Sergey Elibeev. On bent/curved lower control arms, replacement can be attempted without full subframe removal — loosening (not removing) the subframe bolt on one side provides enough clearance. Quoted service labor was 8,000 RUB (RU market, May 2025) (2025-05-04). The Stellox brand part 57-71080a-sx is confirmed as a working lower-arm option 56.

    Front lower-arm bushing brand comparison (Mazda 6 GG cross-fit, D-42mm): reinforcing the Mazda 6 GG cross-fit noted above (part GJ6A34300 GG) — the original X80 lower control-arm bushing bore is 42mm (D-42mm); Mazda 6 GG or first-generation Atenza (2002-2007) 40mm bushings are a direct fit and are generally preferred over buying a single loose bushing. An even better path is sourcing a complete Mazda control arm. Brand notes from the community: Lynx — decent rubber, standard quality; SH Parts — excellent quality, highly recommended; Suffix — poor-quality rubber; STR and 555 — unreliable/poor rubber. Cross-reference part GJ6A34300 GG on used-parts databases (e.g. Drom) for sourcing 7.

    Lower control-arm restyle vs. pre-restyle compatibility — unverified, do not assume: whether the restyled (2018+) and pre-restyle lower control arms are interchangeable is not clearly established in the community; the recommendation is to verify part compatibility with a seller before ordering rather than assuming cross-fit between the two body styles (low confidence — no specific fitment difference was detailed) 8.

    Conical bushing caution — lower ball joint to steering knuckle: when replacing front lower control arms, note there is a conical/tapered bushing installed between the lower ball joint and the steering knuckle. Handle it carefully during disassembly — improper technique can damage the bushing or the knuckle; taking photos before disassembly is recommended 9.

    Front lower arm bushing — repeated failure and Mazda 6 GG cross-reference with dimensions: one owner asked where to source replacement bushings after having replaced the entire front lower control arm the previous year, only for the bushing to fail again 1011. Response: the front lower ("straight") control-arm bushing is available from any shop/marketplace, cross-referencing to the Mazda 6 GG (2002-2008) front lower control-arm bushing; dimension photos were shared for verification before ordering 1213.

    Bushing maintenance at very high mileage — repress vs. full replacement, cost comparison (March 2026): at 260,000+ km, lower front control-arm bushings commonly need attention; the community lays out two paths with concrete March 2026 pricing: (1) repress the existing bushings (перепресовывать) — labor cost ~8,300 RUB (RU market, March 2026), preserves the original arms; or (2) replace the entire arm assembly — cheaper overall (~4,000–5,000 RUB per arm) and faster. Quality warning: budget Chinese arms (JD brand) were reported as poor quality, failing within 2–3 weeks of installation — separate bushing part numbers are hard to source on their own, making full-assembly replacement the more practical DIY route. Symptom: shaking/knocking when turning or over bumps indicates bushing failure 14151617181920.

    Front lower control-arm anatomy — two arm variants, silentblock/ball-joint locations (March 2026): X80 front lower control arms come in two main variants: (1) a transverse short arm with a single silentblock and integrated ball joint, and (2) a longer transverse arm. Both variants have external and internal silentblock mounts, which is where bushing wear concentrates. At 260,000+ km, these silentblocks should be evaluated; professional testing involves inspecting the upper and lower arms with the vehicle on a lift. As with the maintenance-cost note above, complete arm replacement is generally more practical than bushing-only replacement for DIY work 162122232425.

    Front lower control arm — two different-sized bushings, inner vs. outer: the front lower control arm (передний нижний рычаг) uses two differently-sized bushings that must not be confused: FC0134300-2 (inner/internal, сайлентблок внутренний) and FC0134300-1 (outer/external, сайлентблок внешний) — different diameters, different function, and both should be replaced together as a set when worn 262728.

    Inner lower-arm bushing (sway-bar-link bolt point) — part FC0134300-2: the inner bushing at the front lower control arm's inner pivot (bolt position #22 on the exploded suspension diagram) is FAW part FC0134300-2, available from kitzapchasti.ru 2930.

    Front lower control arm bushing — internal diameter mismatch (41mm needed, 40.3mm supplied): buying by part number alone is not reliable — one owner (Egor) ordered the lower control arm bushing and received a part with a 40.3mm internal (bore) diameter, when the X80 needs 41mm; checking other retailers' listings for the same nominal part turned up the same 40.3mm spec across multiple sites, suggesting the mismatch is widespread rather than a one-off packing error 313233. The same thread also covers upper front control-arm bushing wear and a dealer-vs-community sourcing dispute — see the dedicated upper control arm card for that part of the discussion.
