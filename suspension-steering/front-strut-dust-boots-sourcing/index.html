    Front strut dust boots — sourcing

    Tags: #part-number · #mazda-6-compat · #sourcing

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Wrong dust boots installed by service shops cause visible ride-height differences: some service centers install an incorrect dust boot (пыльник) type on the suspension struts — sometimes even after being warned, on the grounds that "the listing says it fits." This produces a visible change in ride height compared to a correctly-fitted boot. In one comparison, Turkish/Sufix-brand suspension springs (14mm total thickness) matched OEM new-spring height correctly when the correct boot was used. Separately, pitted/damaged springs have caused their matching dust boots to break as well. Recommendation: avoid cheap marketplace dust boots of unclear origin; buy from reputable suppliers and verify against a known-good reference before installing. Conflict: one buyer purchased Sufix springs (an alternative brand made for the Chinese market) whose original seller claimed a direct fit but which actually needed a coil cut for modification — OEM springs were eventually preferred over the Sufix ones in that case 12345678.

    Front strut dust boot (piston rod seal) — FAW part 5CA034015, slow EMEX dealer sourcing: the FAW part number for the front strut dust boot/piston rod seal is 5CA034015. Sourcing through FAW dealers (EMEX) has been slow — one owner's order sat stuck on "assembly" status for weeks with no movement. Possible alternative: a Mazda cross-fit part, but this requires verifying the thread pitch on the strut rod before assuming it fits. After the extended EMEX wait, the recommendation is to check local suspension repair shops for a faster-turnaround option or a confirmed Mazda equivalent rather than relying solely on the official channel 91011121314.

    Front dust boots not directly Mazda-cross-fit — X80's extra bearing needs a thicker boot; confirmed alternatives: despite the shared platform lineage, front strut dust boots are not directly interchangeable between the X80 and Mazda 6 (GG) — the X80 has a bearing built into the upper strut support that the GG design lacks, so the X80 needs a dual-part dust boot roughly 10mm thicker than the GG version; fitting a plain GG boot causes improper clearance. Confirmed correct alternatives: SAT STGJ6E34012 or Katsuro KAT19706MAZ (rubber support) combined with a Masuma MAB1062 dust boot, or the complete kit Febest MZSH-BGYFKIT (includes 2 dust boots + 2 bump stops). PR7029 was also mentioned as a potential match, unconfirmed. Sourcing difficulty: many of these parts show as out of stock or with 8–12 day delivery windows — plan ahead rather than ordering when a boot has already failed 15161718192021222324252627.

    Kits and strut-top parts. A kit of 2 dust boots and 2 bump stops is listed as KYB 910107 or Monroe PK157. Spring-seat spacers are Katsuro KAT19706MAZ or SAT STGJ6E34012, but their codes carry Mazda markers, so fitment must be checked. A member suggested PR7029 for the boot, while another owner said the boot in the shared photo is the wrong one.282920 The Kitparts listing for the front strut support bearing 5CA03438YA starts at 5,174 RUB (RU market, October 2025).30 A Masuma SAM-4101 front strut mount (опора амортизатора) for the Mazda 6 2002-07 is listed on Ozon; the post does not state fitment to the X80.31
