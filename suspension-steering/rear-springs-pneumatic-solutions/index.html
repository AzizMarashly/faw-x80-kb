    Rear springs — failure and pneumatic fixes

    Tags: #springs · #price · #mazda-6-compat

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Summary: the X80's stock rear springs are a known weak point (cracked or sagging coils, bottoming out under load). The community's fixes fall into four groups: replacement springs from other cars (Mazda 6, CX-5, Kia Sorento and others), spacers under the springs for lift, air bellows (пневмобаллоны) inside the coils, and diagnosis of what actually failed. The hub keeps the core failure, sourcing and part-number facts; the detail sits in the sibling cards listed at the bottom.

    Failure pattern: rear springs have a reputation for weak reliability. One owner had both rear springs fail at 75,000 km. Spec for reference: 13.7mm wire thickness (community treats ~13mm+ as acceptable), 6–7 coil turns per side. Overloading — e.g. 4 people plus cargo in a vehicle rated for a lighter load per its registration — contributes to premature failure 1.

    OEM availability/cost: original rear springs were available (Oct 2024) at the FAW official dealership in Moscow (Autogermés, Sormovskaya branch, reachable via the main website → transfer to parts department → reserve). Stock was limited — 6 units found in October 2024 — and reported to sell out quickly 2.

    Aftermarket alternative: Chinese aftermarket springs from Kitparts.ru, ~3,310 RUB (RU market, October 2024) per pair (left/right identical), matching the original 13.7mm rod diameter. Inferior-quality copies (12.6–12.8mm rod) reportedly fail within 1.5–3 months. Other cross-fit sources: Mazda OEM springs (16mm rod, compatible) and Honda Stepwgn springs (16mm rod, need a minor custom spacer with rubber pads to fit). Toyota/Honda alternatives beyond the Stepwgn require significant customization and are not recommended 3.

    Disagreement — springs alone vs. spacers vs. pneumatic bellows: one contributor (Олег Белоусов) recommends pneumatic air-spring bellows (Belarusian brand) as the primary fix over stronger springs; others argue 2cm spacers are essential for proper fitment regardless of spring choice. Two owners (Сергей Куликов, Олег) both experienced failure with weak aftermarket springs, but had success either with original springs plus pneumatic bellows, or with Mazda donor parts 3.

    OEM rear spring part number — 5CA028011: the rear spring's own OEM FAW part number, referenced in catalogs for all X80 model years (2013–2021), is 5CA028011; cross-brand analogs sold under this listing include SF2598 / ZC6416H / ST119065R (see the cross-brand options documented elsewhere on this card) 4. The community's earliest recorded mention of the Kayaba/KYB 6416 cross-brand rear spring/shock option (see the detailed KYB RH6416 wire-thickness comparison elsewhere on this card) dates back to an early, terse report simply citing "Kayaba 6416" for the rear springs 5.

    Reference. Victor's Drive2 post "14. Эпопея с задними пружинами и амортизаторами" (rear springs and shocks saga) documents his work in detail: https://www.drive2.ru/l/741593106217980020/ 6.
