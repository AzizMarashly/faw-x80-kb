    Ownership reliability overview vs. Mazda 6

    Tags: #reliability · #mazda-6-compat

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    Community consensus: overall the X80 is regarded as "a good car" ("хорошая машина") with some "quirks" and "nuances." Main recurring themes: (1) non-standard crankcase ventilation design, (2) electronic-throttle response lag tied to ECU integration, (3) hot-weather engine performance degradation, (4) A/C compressor vibration. Many owners upgrade suspension components (shocks, control arms) using Mazda 6 GG parts. The community is described as experienced and willing to share detailed technical knowledge.123

    Failure-rate comparison vs. Mazda 6 (same underlying Mazda L-series engine/platform, but the FAW X80 shows shorter service intervals on several shared components, per one long-time owner's comparison of Drive2 and other forums): - Upper engine mount: Mazda 60–110K km vs. FAW 30–60K km - Front upper-arm bushings (сайлентблоки): Mazda from ~80K km vs. FAW from 20–60K km (sometimes noisy even when new) - Front stabilizer-link mounts: Mazda from ~100K km vs. FAW from ~20K km - Steering-rack knock: Mazda from ~60K km vs. FAW from ~80K km (here FAW reported as lasting longer) - Lower control arm ("рыбка"/"гитара"): Mazda after ~100K km vs. FAW cases reported after only ~30K km - Temperature-sensor housing: Mazda from ~30–40K km, FAW is "a rare lottery" (variable, not consistently earlier or later) - RPM surging/hunting: Mazda's earliest reported case ~180K km (typically after 250K km); not yet observed on FAW at time of report - Fuel pump: reported worse (shorter life) on FAW than Mazda's 150K+ km benchmark - Water intrusion into the driver/passenger footwell — an issue specific to FAW, not seen on Mazda - Various electrical faults, windshield leaks, early rear-spring sagging, and weaker AC cooling than on the Mazda-sourced 09G transmission — all reported as more frequent on FAW than on the donor Mazda 6.4

    Independent ownership report (second Telegram group): frequent non-critical issues include (1) thermostat requiring replacement with a quality aftermarket part, (2) water intrusion under the floor carpets (driver/passenger side) — common but solvable, (3) front/rear coil-spring breakage affecting many units, attributed to weak Chinese-OEM springs, (4) winter brake rattle/squeal affecting nearly all units and difficult to eliminate completely. Overall assessment from this report: a robust and reliable Chinese vehicle with extensive Mazda 6 parts compatibility and readily available service parts. Fuel economy cited: manual transmission 7–11 L/100km, automatic 11+ L/100km. Diagnostic scanners may not support advanced diagnostics at all service centers. Illustrative case: an owner with 193,000 km and 4 accidents still owns and intends to keep the vehicle.56789101112131415
