    Water leaks — door seals and body sealing

    Tags: #water-ingress

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    Silicone sealant along hood/window/door seams — simple DIY prevention: apply silicone sealant (specifically VMPAUTO 2401 silicone, 30ml bottle) along the hood edges, window seals, and door-frame gaps to stop water reaching the cabin and soaking the floor carpets — particularly important on the driver and passenger sides near the windshield. One owner reported dry carpets all spring after a single application 12345.

    Root-cause explanation and DIY channel fix — door-frame seal degradation (universal, eventual issue): root cause identified via video demonstration: the original door-frame rubber seals degrade by around year 3, crumbling and losing their water-blocking ability. Water path: the roof-edge panel angles inward, collecting water and channeling it toward the cabin; the factory drain gutter at that point is too narrow, and HVAC intake suction pulls water in through it regardless of gutter capacity. Replacement seals (e.g. sourced from a Ford Fiesta) only last 6–12 months before the same failure recurs. DIY mitigation demonstrated on video: cut a strip of sheet metal from an aluminum beverage can, attach it along the water-flow line with double-sided tape plus silicone sealant, creating an improvised gutter/ channel that redirects water away from the HVAC intake. Community consensus: this problem will eventually affect all X80s to some degree, sooner or later — it's a design-level weak point, not a one-off manufacturing defect 6789101112.

    Floor-mat clip gaps as a water/electrical-short source: water can leak into the cabin through inadequate sealing around the floor-mat attachment clip areas — these clips have large gaps that let water and sand enter during winter. This was the source of an electrical short in the GLONASS module's wiring when left unsealed. Fix: open the mounting clips, add rubber washers, and use adhesive for better sealing 13.

    Underbody drain-plug maintenance: rubber plugs in the underbody drain holes can dry out over time and allow water to enter the cabin. Fix: replace the rubber plugs with new ones, or seal the affected areas with vibration-damping material. This is a common maintenance item that should be checked periodically 14151617181920.

    Water under driver floor mat — non-hermetic internal seams: water collected under the driver's floor mat after rain, traced to non-hermetic internal body seams and mounting holes that were only sealed with tape and paint from the factory instead of proper sealant. Fix: identify and re-seal all affected seams with hermetic sealant (герметизация швов); once sealed, drainage normalizes and the mat stays dry 21.

    Water pooling in cabin corners — drainage-hole checklist (rear, near fuel-tank lever): water pooling in cabin corners, especially the rear area near the fuel-tank release lever, is a known issue with several contributing causes: (1) uncleaned door drainage holes and heater air-intake drainage holes; (2) defective door seal gaskets; (3) water entering through the heater air intake (a pattern also seen on other models). Remedy: inspect and clean all drainage holes, check door seals, dry the interior thoroughly with a vacuum and towels/newspaper, and verify the BSM (body/comfort control module) hasn't been flooded, since it controls many vehicle systems. The pre-facelift model reportedly has more drainage issues than the facelift version 222324252627282930.

    Water ingress through door-sill plastic plugs (front passenger): water entering the cabin at the front-passenger sill area has been traced to plastic plugs/stoppers at the bottom of the door-sill cavities that don't seal properly. Diagnosis: remove the plastic undercarriage (belly-pan) trim to access the inside of the sills from underneath the car — water flows through these plug apertures into the interior. Fix: inspect the openings, clean them, and ensure the plug apertures are properly sealed 3132333435363738.

    Water under driver's pedals — firewall/bulkhead seal failure (possible warranty defect): separately, water ingress under the driver's pedals has also been attributed to a failed seal at the bulkhead between the driver's footwell and the engine bay — repair requires replacing the sealing gasket at that firewall interface. This may be a factory manufacturing defect and could be covered under warranty on newer cars, unlike the general wear-based leak causes documented elsewhere in this card 394041.

    Water intrusion under carpets — left fender/wing seam (common, not fully diagnosed): water appears under the driver and passenger floor carpets after parking in rain/snow or after washing, traced to the area of a seam at the top of the left fender/wing. The community has not fully pinned down the exact ingress path; described as a "common issue, solvable but not simple" — solving it requires identifying the precise leak source and sealing the seams or checking door/body gasket integrity. Affects multiple owners 42434445.

    See also: Water leaks — fender and cabin ingress; Water leaks — trunk and rear door; Water leaks — footwell diagnosis and A/C drain.
