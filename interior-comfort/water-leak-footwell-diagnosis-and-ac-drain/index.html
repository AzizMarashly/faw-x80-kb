    Water leaks — footwell diagnosis and A/C drain

    Tags: #water-ingress

    Passenger-side floor water — multiple candidate sources: water entering the passenger-side floor can come from several independent points: the A/C drain-tube angle (can back-flow water while driving), windshield/side-window seals, door drains, hood drain holes, a rubbing brake line, heater-core leaks, or seams in the engine-bay motor shield. Facelift/restyle models lack a manual A/C drain-angle adjustment (present on earlier models). Diagnostic tip: check the cabin (saloon) air filter — if it's wet, the water is most likely coming from the evaporator/A/C drain path rather than door or window seals. Drain tubes should have a small protective bend built in to stop wind-driven rain from being pushed back up into them 12345678910111213.

    Wet driver-side carpet — possible causes checklist: water reaching carpets (especially the driver's floor mat) in rain may trace back to: (1) degraded seal on the intake tube entering the cabin; (2) a blocked drainage hole under the hood/windshield area; (3) failed windshield sealant; (4) deteriorated body-panel seals. Check hood-edge seals, verify the drainage holes under the cowl are clear, and inspect windshield and door seals — the pattern tends to track seasonal weather 14151617.

    Systematic water-leak troubleshooting — full DIY fix sequence: this is a very common issue affecting most X80s to some degree, and several independent fixes are often needed together rather than one single cause: (1) extend the drain lines inside the door pillars; (2) remove sharp corners/kinks along drain paths so water flows freely; (3) reseal weatherstripping around the roof panel edge; (4) raise the intake duct under the hood, adding a small partition wall to redirect water away; (5) apply masking tape over the intake opening as a temporary water-diverter; (6) extend the A/C drain tube and seal the joint; (7) spot-seal remaining gaps with black caulk. This is a substantial, multi-step job, but owners who completed the full sequence reported achieving a genuinely dry cabin afterward 18192021222324252627282930.

    Wet floor under front seats — drainage-pipe disconnection check: if experiencing wet floors under the driver and/or passenger seats, the likely cause is the underbody drainage system. Check the drainage-pipe connections at the floor on both the driver and passenger sides; these pipes can become disconnected or blocked, letting water accumulate inside. Inspect and reconnect any loose drainage joints found 3132333435.

    Three-zone cabin water-intrusion summary (driver footwell, passenger footwell, trunk): a comprehensive report identifies three primary leak zones: (1) driver footwell — moisture on the GNSS/telematics connector under the driver-side carpet, which is a critical failure mode since it can disable the transmission ECU; (2) passenger footwell — water seepage through the seat-rail/sliding area; (3) trunk — accumulation despite the drain fitting. Root causes cited: (a) windshield/hood seal degradation; (b) liftgate drain-fitting issues; (c) damaged hood drip channels; (d) weak interior-panel fasteners at the door/window-frame interface; (e) low/degraded window weatherstripping. One owner at 150,000 km reports ongoing monitoring of these zones; another reports water damage required replacing the transmission ECU. Recommended fix: regular interior inspection, check the liftgate drain plug, inspect hood seals, and seal any visible gaps 36373839404142434445464748.

    Water under brake pedal/dashboard — drain-tube disconnection: water collecting under the pedals and on the cabin floor is commonly caused by the drainage tube under the dashboard, near the brake pedal (близ педали тормоза), coming loose from the body. Fix: extend the silicone drainage tube with a section of copper pipe and route it properly to drain outside the cabin, preventing further accumulation 49.

    Interior drainage system — quick leak-location reference: water leaking under the carpets typically flows to two main drain points: (1) front left, under the driver's feet, and front right, under the passenger's feet (plastic clip/drain point); (2) a rear drain access point also exists. All drain tubes route from the windshield cowl area downward. This drainage issue has reportedly been partially resolved on newer restyle models via factory improvements 5051525354.

    Water pooling under the driver's feet — early diagnosis checklist: water pooling in the driver-side footwell is a long-known issue on the X80. Community-recommended diagnosis steps: (1) check the hood drain system — make sure it's clear and functioning; (2) inspect the engine-compartment panels on both sides of the engine (front panel and side panels) for water seepage or poor seals; (3) check the air intake area on the right side under the hood for water intrusion. If water still accumulates after these checks, the cause may be door seals, the sunroof drain (if equipped), or a windshield-gasket leak 55565758.

    Water under driver's pedals — recirculation-mode mitigation: switching the HVAC intake from recirculation to fresh/outside-air mode reduces moisture accumulation under the driver's pedals, since it brings in drier external air instead of recirculating cabin humidity. A simple, no-repair mitigation rather than a root-cause fix 59.
