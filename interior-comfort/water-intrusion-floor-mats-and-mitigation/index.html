    Water intrusion under floor mats

    Tags: #sunroof · #mazda-6-compat · #conflict

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    Community members disagree on this topic — both positions are reported.

    Water pooling under the driver's and front-passenger floor mats is one of the most frequently repeated complaints in the community, but it does not affect every car and its severity varies widely.

    Root cause and scope: the issue is inherited from the Mazda 6 platform design — the A/C condensate drain and sunroof drain routing can allow water to collect in the front footwells instead of draining outside the cabin. Community estimates put the affected share at roughly 10% of vehicles, not universal. It is fixable by extending/rerouting the drain hoses and making sure the drain tubes are actually connected (a common assembly-line omission) 123.

    2019 automatic — mitigation via aftermarket floor mats: one 2019 automatic-transmission owner had water pooling in the rear cabin area specifically in winter. Switching from the stock factory floor mats to Evo-brand mats with raised borders ("с бортами") resolved the problem, suggesting the water had been entering through the factory mats' fastener holes rather than a structural leak. No electrical issues were reported in this case, and the owner's experience points to inadequate splash protection in the factory mat design as at least part of the problem 4.

    Conflict — the problem is inconsistent across the fleet, even on restyled (2021+) models: community reports diverge sharply. Some owners on restyled (2021+) cars report they have never had any water problems at all, even in very heavy rain 5. One such owner draws a comparison to a Chevrolet Cruze they'd previously owned that suffered severe sunroof-drain flooding, and notes the whole community had reported the same Cruze issue — implying the X80's sunroof drain, while imperfect, is less failure-prone by comparison 6. Another owner offers a rule of thumb: if the interior stays dry and electrics behave normally even without rain, either a previous owner already fixed the underlying issue, or that particular unit was simply never one of the "problem" cars to begin with 7. A different owner, who had owned their (evidently problem-affected) car for only a month to six weeks and had washed it a couple of times with a Kärcher pressure washer, offers a data point on the other side of the conflict, suggesting some cars leak almost immediately under pressure-washing or heavy rain regardless of age 8. The community's overall read: first-owner care and unit-to-unit factory variation (see the assembly-quality card) matter more than model year or restyle status in determining whether a given car will leak.
