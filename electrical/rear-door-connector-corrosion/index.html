    Rear door connector pin corrosion

    Tags: #corrosion · #mirror · #turn-signal · #part-number

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Owners report that the left rear door connector is a recurring weak point on the X80: oxidation/corrosion in its pins causes intermittent failures across several unrelated-seeming systems at once — door-lock sensors, power windows (auto-down/up mode not learning), mirrors (working only right at startup), and turn-signal relays. Some pins may be physically broken or corroded beyond simple cleaning. The rear driver-side door is specifically flagged as particularly prone to this 12 345678910.

    Fix options, from cheapest to most reliable. (1) Remove the connector and clean pins with electrical contact cleaner — works for mild oxidation. (2) Replace damaged pins individually — pin stock in the correct size is hard to source; Mazda CX-series equivalents are slightly larger and don't fit the thin connector walls, so don't assume cross-compatibility. (3) Improvise a fix with a piece of solder or wire to take up slack in a loose/worn pin 1245 679.

    Full connector replacement and part numbers. For a permanent fix rather than pin-by-pin repair: (1) buy a new connector, part 5CA067210B (~1000 RUB, RU market, September 2025) — this resolves window-regulator and door-open-signal failures caused by pin corrosion on the rear left door specifically. (2) Alternative, free but more labor: extract the corroded pin and bypass it with a direct wire run through the door-harness connector (~20 minutes, permanent against future oxidation at that specific point, though it doesn't replace the whole connector). (3) For the trunk/boot door specifically (not a regular door), the equivalent connector part is 5CA267SH2B (~1000 RUB, RU market, September 2025) — confirm which door (left/right, regular vs. trunk) before ordering, since the part numbers are door-specific 1112131415161718 1920212223.

    Intermittent rear door won't open — corrosion vs. aftermarket-alarm interference (unresolved which). A separate, partially-overlapping report: the rear door stopped opening reliably (worked intermittently, notably sometimes triggered back into working by using the wipers first) 24. First mechanic consulted made things worse by disassembling the lock without properly diagnosing it; a second visit tested the lock on a bench battery and found it functional in isolation, pointing away from the lock mechanism itself and toward the wiring/connector path 25. Suspected cause: oxidized contacts earlier in the same circuit that also carries power to the wipers — since running the wipers sometimes "unstuck" the door, the wiper circuit and door-lock circuit may share a corroded junction upstream of both 2627. Fuses for wipers and central locking were all visually intact, but a fuse can fail without visible damage, so replacement/swap-testing was still recommended 28. Conflict: whether an aftermarket alarm is the actual root cause, versus plain connector oxidation, was not resolved in the thread — one suggestion was to try resetting the alarm via its service button, or disconnect the battery for 10+ minutes as a general reset, before further disassembly 2930.
