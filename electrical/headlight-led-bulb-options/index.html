    Headlight LED bulb conversion options

    Tags: #headlight

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    More bulb options and pricing (Aug 2025, RU market). DawnKnight K7C 150W 9012 (HIR2) 6000K and MTF Light HS12H2 (HIR2 Standart+30%) both available on Ozon; MTF Light's price dropped from 2×750 RUB to ~840 RUB per bulb during this window 1234 5.

    HIR2 (9012) confirmed as the socket standard, with a TNP option. Reconfirming the HIR2/9012 socket noted elsewhere: TNP LED BRENDY 9012 HIR2 bulbs (sold on Wildberries, among other bright options) gave one owner excellent night visibility; caution — some units arrive defective, so request a visual inspection from the seller before payment 6789.

    LED 360° conversion — spec and heat. LED 360° conversion lamps (8000K color temperature) run at 85°C normally, with diodes rated for continuous 105°C — example brand: Carshark 12V, sold as a 2-pack. This level of heat is normal and not a concern; fans on the unit may feel warm, which is expected 10111213.

    Brand comparison — 360° vs directional. Multiple LED options exist: 360° rotation bulbs (adjustable direction after installation) vs. traditional fixed-focus bulbs 1415. Tested brands: Carshark 360° (lower performance on the highway) 1617; Philips (well-regarded) 1819; DawnKnight (good light output but dark spots noted, from its flat-diode design) 2021; AILEO 360° (tunable 3000K–6500K color temperature, 80W, rated highest among those tried) 222324. Prices are variable and some listings drop ~50% shortly after purchase 2526. The flat-diode design can cause dark spots specifically in lens-equipped headlights; proper adjustment can mitigate this 27282930 3132333435363738 3940.

    Conflict: owner experiences diverge — some rate 360° LEDs as giving superior light output, others prefer Philips or stock halogen; reported results also depend on individual fuse/wiring quality 1634.

    Real-world upgrade result. Stock halogen bulbs were reported to give very poor night visibility ("couldn't see the road at night") 41. DawnKnight 12V LED bulbs, bought from Ozon (product 842925366), gave a large improvement — full road visibility in rain and on the highway 4243. A side-by-side comparison of HIR2 LED vs. stock halogen on one side of the car showed a dramatic brightness difference 4445. The one minor cosmetic downside on highway night driving: two bright spots visible from the LED/HID units in front of the hood.

    Bulb options and pricing (Dec 2024, RU market). MTF Light halogen (Ozon) — OEM-equivalent 4647. Carshark 360° warm 4300K LED — budget option, ~2000 RUB (RU market, December 2024), a solid upgrade over halogen 4849. Red Stil LED — an alternative LED option (Ozon) 50. MTF Light Cyber Light HIR2 (9012) 6000K — premium LED option (WildBerries), ~5000+ RUB, praised by auto-lighting studios 5152. HB4 is a cheaper alternative to HIR2 where the sockets have already been modified. Any LED lamp must include a built-in load resistor ("обманка"/decoder) to avoid dashboard errors; cheap LEDs often lack this, causing a permanent low-brightness glow and error codes.

    Connector types — HIR2 vs HB4. HIR2 connectors have a notch/key pin; HB4 connectors are smooth without it, so HB4 bulbs will not fit a standard HIR2 socket without modification 5354. If the original sockets have already been replaced (e.g. after melting from overheating), both HB4 and HIR2 may fit the replacement socket. HB4 is significantly cheaper (~400–600 RUB, RU market, December 2024) than HIR2 (~2000+ RUB pre-2024, ~5000 RUB for current premium LED units, RU market, December 2024) 55.

    More brand/pricing data points (Dec 2025). DawnKnight K7C and K9C Pro (H7/H4/H11 sockets, copper cooling tubes) continue to come up as an option, though one owner's K7C produced an uneven light pattern with dark spots despite correct installation — consistent with the flat-diode dark-spot issue already noted above; he ultimately swapped to a 360-degree circular LED design instead and had no further issue 5657585960. K9C Pro was seen on AliExpress at ~2,060 RUB (RU market, December 2025) with a 50% discount during this window 57. Wattage vs. lumens — a recurring buyer confusion. Stock halogen bulbs are rated 55W; a 20W rating on an LED replacement refers to power consumption, not light output — lumens (light output) is the metric that actually determines brightness, and a lower-wattage LED can still out-light a 55W halogen because LEDs are far more efficient per watt 616263. After any LED headlight install, booking a proper beam-adjustment/alignment at a shop (~3,000+ RUB) is recommended, since LED conversions commonly need re-aiming even when the bulb itself fits correctly 60.

    HB4 socket confirmed for low beam, plus HB4→HIR2 conversion notes. The FAW X80's low-beam headlight socket is HB4 6465. Replacement HB4 connectors are available through ordinary auto-parts shops 6667. Confirming and extending the HIR2-vs-HB4 connector-shape difference documented above: HB4 and HIR2 sockets are close but not identical — converting an HB4 socket to accept an HIR2 bulb requires filing/trimming a small key/tab on the socket to make it fit 6869. A salvageable option when swapping to LED: the old HB4 connector/pigtail can be cut and rewired directly onto new LED bulb leads rather than buying a separate adapter harness, if the LED bulb is a bare halogen-style (not integrated-connector) design 707172.

    HIR2 (9012) LED installation and aiming — a consolidated procedure. Combining several installation reports into one sequence for a 9012/HIR2 LED swap (this batch centers on a 185W-equivalent "DLED premium" listing on Ozon, ~2,000–3,000 RUB/pair, RU market, December 2025) 73: (1) verify bulb polarity/ connector orientation before inserting — the connector is keyed but double-check anyway 74757677; (2) insert with the ground wire pointing DOWN — this is called out as critical for correct beam shape, distinct from the diode-orientation issue covered in the headlight-adjustment card 7879; (3) if one side's beam looks wrong after install, suspect the LED diode array being mounted backwards relative to the other side — compare orientation side-to-side rather than assuming a bad bulb 8081; (4) after installation, use the interior aim-height control (left of the steering wheel) to set the beam height, and check for a rotatable adjustment shim/ring on the bulb's base itself that lets you fine-tune direction independent of the bulb's fixed diode orientation 8283 84858687888990 9192; (5) keep the wiring routed neatly and disconnect the negative battery terminal before starting the install; test at low speed/in a controlled area before highway driving 9394. Despite the multi-step process, LED bulbs are consistently reported as a significant illumination improvement over halogen once correctly installed and aimed — see the headlight-adjustment card for the beam- pattern troubleshooting steps if the result still isn't right after following this sequence.

    Headlight connector pin-count query — verify before ordering from AliExpress. A minor, low-confidence but practical note: a buyer sourcing a pre-facelift headlight assembly from AliExpress couldn't tell from the listing whether the connector was 11-pin or 12-pin, and the community's answer was that this needs to be physically verified on the actual vehicle before ordering rather than assumed from a generic listing photo — connector pin-count on AliExpress listings for this car isn't reliably accurate 959697.

    K7C (HIR2 9012) LED bulb spec and color temperature. The FAW X80 uses K7C-type (HIR2 9012) bulbs for its headlights. LED replacements are available in 4300K (warmer, yellowish) and 6000K (cooler, bluish) color temperatures. Color temperature (K) affects only the color of the light, not brightness (lumens) — both variants deliver the same light output. Community consensus favors 4300K for reduced glare to oncoming drivers; 6000K is considered too bright/harsh by comparison. The DawnKnight brand (models DK_LED_K7C_9012_4300K and the 6000K variant) is available on the Ozon marketplace 9899100101102103 104105106.

    General LED headlight-bulb upgrade — improved brightness, no housing mods. Aftermarket LED headlight bulbs, more broadly, provide significantly improved brightness over the stock bulbs and are a direct replacement with no housing modification needed. Professional beam realignment is recommended after installation. Available on the Ozon marketplace 107108.

    Three-tier options summary, with the professional-conversion tier not covered elsewhere in this card. A separate thread summarizes the choices at three price/quality tiers, adding a tier not otherwise documented here: (1) cheap LED drop-in bulbs (~3,000-5,000 RUB, RU market, September 2024) — often poor color rendition and imprecise fitment, not generally recommended; (2) branded aftermarket LEDs (Carshark, DawnKnight, and OLED variants on Ozon) — mixed results, brightness varies by unit, and even when output is good it's often not dramatically better than halogen; expect 4300-6000K color temperatures, consistent with the ranges documented elsewhere in this card; (3) a full professional headlight conversion (~30,000 RUB, RU market, September 2024) — the OEM optic is removed and a diode/HID unit is professionally installed inside the lens itself, with some shops offering a lifetime warranty and notably better light quality than a simple bulb swap. Regardless of which tier is chosen, the recurring safety caveat applies: headlights must be professionally aimed (регулировка СТГ) after any bulb/conversion change, since white LEDs in a misaligned housing blind oncoming traffic 109110111112113114115116 117118119120121122123.

    Further confirmations from a second Telegram group — pricing, socket type, and the 360°-vs-2-sided trade-off. A separate community group independently reconfirms and adds pricing/technical detail to the K7C (HIR2/9012) picture documented above. Socket/bulb type: FAW X80 headlamps use the HIR2 (9012) socket; K7C-branded LED bulbs are the widely-recommended, cost-effective choice here, sold on Ozon at roughly 2,500 RUB per pair (RU market, March 2026) and rated as giving good light output for the price 124125126127128129. A broader options round-up from the same group largely matches the tiered summary above — cheap drop-ins, branded LED (Dawnknight K7C/K9C among them), and a full conversion — with the same emphasis on professional beam aiming afterward 130131132133134135136137 138139140141142143144145146147 148149. Specific product data: HB4-fitment white 6000K LED bulbs, 19,000-lumen rated, CSP chips, 106W (pair) — Dawnknight K7C and K9C models specifically recommended for the X80 (product link: https://ozon.ru/t/jhFxooM) 132134145146148. More detailed part numbers: DawnKnight K7C LEDs in 4300K or 6000K, rated 40W, 8000lm output — part numbers DK_LED_K7C_H1_6000K and DK_LED_K7C_9012_4300K; a fallback option is filing down one connector lobe on an HB3/HB4 bulb to force-fit the HIR2 socket. Installation takes 5-10 minutes: remove the old bulb, fit the retaining ring separately, then insert the new lamp 150151152153154155 156157158159160161162163164 165166167168169170.

    360°-vs-2-sided LED technology comparison, with the dark-zone trade-off quantified. Adding specific angular figures to the 360° vs directional comparison already documented in this card: 2-sided lamps like the K7C emit across roughly 170-175 degrees, which creates a dark zone directly ahead/below on the road once installed — its main advantages are low cost (~1,500-2,000 RUB, RU market, January 2026) and simple installation. 360-degree lamps (8+ matrix diode designs) emit across the full circle, eliminating that dark zone entirely, at a higher cost (~2,500-3,000 RUB or more, RU market, January 2026), a wider mounting envelope that needs fine adjustment after install, and — even among 360° lamps — differing matrix counts (6 vs 8) produce different light-overlap patterns. Despite the optical downside, K7C-type lamps remain the popular default because of their cost-performance ratio 171172173174175176 177178.

    Stock low-beam judged inadequate at night — another independent 9012/HIR2 4300K upgrade report. A separate owner reported the stock low beams give insufficient night illumination (possibly because previous owners had swapped in mismatched bulbs), forcing them to run the front fog lights as a supplement just to see 179180. The recommended fix: a 9012 HIR2 LED upgrade — one example cited was "TNP Brendy" (TNP LED, sold on Wildberries) with active cooling 181; another owner linked a similar 9012 HIR2 LED listing on Ozon.ru that they had installed and aimed on a proper alignment stand 182. Color-temperature guidance, reinforcing the 4300K-vs-6000K consensus already documented above: 6000K was described as "like welding" (too much glare), while 4300K doesn't blind oncoming traffic and performs noticeably better in bad/wet weather; when the low beams are paired with the fog lights running, the combination gives a daytime-like appearance and excellent road coverage 183. As with every LED upgrade in this card: get the beam re-aimed at a service center after installation, since a bright but misaimed beam blinds oncoming drivers rather than helping the owner see better.

    Low-beam socket dispute and colour temperature (September 2026, RU market). One owner states the X80 low-beam socket is HIR2 (9012) only, not HB4 or HB3, and asks whether the retaining-claw geometry of an HB4 bulb really matches; another owner thinks HB4 looks identical and has seen an HB4 listing with the same base, and a third notes that universal HB4/HB5/HIR2 LED bulbs now exist and that earlier owners adapted HB5 bulbs 184185186. This disagrees with the reports above that the low beam takes an HB4 socket; both positions are reported as stated.

    Owners fitting DawnKnight K7C 9012 (HIR2) LED bulbs report good light in snow and rain 187188. One owner bought cheap look-alike units on Ozon that were not the brand, returned them and bought a dearer pair that matched the listing 189. Listings in September 2026 showed the 6000K K7C HIR2/9012 pair at ~2,171 RUB and ~2,829 RUB, and a 6000K HIR2 unit at ~2,889 RUB (RU market, September 2026); the 4300K 9012 version was sold out 190187191. Listing text: 25,000 lm, 110 W, built-in CANBUS decoder, 12-month warranty.

    On colour temperature, one owner says 4300K is warmer, close to halogen, and dazzles oncoming drivers less in rain and fog, while 6000K looks blinding; another owner running 4300K agrees it is enough 192193. An owner running 6000K argues that lens quality and beam pattern matter more than colour 194. He recalls paying ~3,000 RUB for an X80 LED set (RU market, September 2026) against ~8,000 RUB for bulbs for his earlier Chery Tiggo 194. One member claimed that a canbus box is only for the car radio and modern LED bulbs need nothing extra; nobody confirmed it, and it conflicts with the usual purpose of a canbus decoder (preventing bulb-error warnings and flicker) - the DawnKnight listings state the decoder is built in, so check before buying 195.
