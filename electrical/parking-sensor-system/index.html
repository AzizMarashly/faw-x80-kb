    Parking sensor (parktronic) system

    Tags: #parking-sensor · #can-bus · #part-number · #reliability

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    CAN-bus integration with aftermarket Android head units (manual transmission). On X80s with manual transmission and an aftermarket Android head unit, the parking-sensor sound may work while the visual display on the head-unit screen does not — this needs CAN-bus wiring harness modification, or a separate dedicated signal wire, since manual cars lack the OEM reverse-gear CAN signal that automatics provide for the head unit. The correct fix varies by head-unit model; some owners solved it via a diagnostic-schematic wire-tapping method (connecting the sensor's ground/signal to specific pins on the CAN connector). Appears manufacturer/firmware-dependent rather than having one universal fix 12345678 910.

    Rear central sensor part number and no-bumper-removal replacement. Central rear parking-sensor part code: 5GA1-79120, available on AliExpress for roughly 900 RUB (RU market, August 2025). Installation tip: central sensors can be replaced without removing the bumper if you have a long-reach tool; the fix requires repositioning the silicone grommet seal correctly 11.

    Architecture. The four parking sensors connect to a dedicated parking radar control unit (referred to as "R7" in one diagnostic session) over the CAN data bus 121314. The control unit gets its activation signal from the transmission (reverse-gear detection), and exchanges data with the head unit over CAN — including whether the control unit itself is present/responding and its configuration 141516. The rear-view camera is wired to the infotainment head unit (ГУ) separately from the parking-sensor circuit, so a parking-sensor fault does not affect the camera 1217 18.

    Single-sensor failure takes down the whole system. On the factory (OEM) system, if just one of the four sensors fails, the entire parking system reports a fault rather than simply disabling that one sensor — the remaining working sensors are detected, but the system reports a malfunction as a whole 1920. One owner had two sensors fail at once and had to order both from the dealer before the system worked again 21. Sensor failures across the roughly five-year OEM warranty period are common, with reports of sensors failing sequentially rather than all at once, starting as early as year 1 and continuing to year 3–4 2021. Once replaced with functioning sensors, the system returns to normal operation 21. If replacing a non-OEM/aftermarket head unit with the original sensors, CAN-bus compatibility must also be considered, since the head unit is part of the same data exchange 18.

    Part number. OEM parking sensor part number: 5CA17912002, available from retailers such as EMEX and Autodoc 2223. Advice before ordering: run diagnostics first to confirm which specific sensor(s) are actually faulty before buying replacements 24. The sensors are ultrasonic type; a low-tech diagnostic trick some owners use is listening for their emission with an audio recorder's microphone, which can pick up the ultrasonic pulses to confirm whether a given sensor is firing at all.

    Repeated failure after car washes — moisture/contact-oxidation root cause and fixes. A separate, well-documented failure pattern: parking sensors repeatedly fail (or produce false readings) after car washes. Root cause is moisture causing oxidation on the sensor's or connector's contacts. One owner reported replacing a sensor for roughly the fourth time in 2.5 years due to this recurring pattern 25. Recommended fixes, in order of thoroughness: (1) remove the affected sensor(s) and dry completely; (2) protect the connection with aquarium-grade silicone sealant, or heat-shrink tubing over the joint; (3) clean the connector with electrical contact cleaner (обезжирка) before resealing; (4) apply dielectric grease to the contacts for ongoing moisture resistance 2627. Under warranty, one owner had all 4 sensors replaced at some point over 5 years with no further issues since — suggesting warranty replacement can be a durable fix if available, versus repeated DIY patch jobs 28. Without warranty, the warranty repair process itself can be slow (one report: 4 hours of diagnosis plus a month's wait for parts, then 3 hours of installation) 29. Cost data point: a single replacement sensor plus installation ran about 750 RUB (sensor) + 1000 RUB (labor) (RU market, May 2024) for one owner who opted to just keep replacing individual sensors as they failed rather than pursue the more thorough moisture-proofing fix above 30. If sensors keep failing every 2-3 years despite basic care, consider the sealant/dielectric-grease approach above rather than repeat bare replacements.

    PDC (park distance control) module location. The PDC/парктроник control module is located in the trunk on the right side, mounted between the exhaust system and the battery area. It's accessible from inside the trunk; typical removal time is roughly 1 hour. It's mounted on a 6mm threaded boss with a plastic nut (wide flange), and those threads are fragile and commonly get damaged by inexperienced removal attempts. Note: the parking sensors themselves continue to function with ignition on, but the module can show power-supply issues while the engine is actually running — suspected to be generator ripple/voltage noise on that circuit 3132333435 36.

    PDC sensor bench-testing with a multimeter. Individual parking sensors can be tested with a digital multimeter set to the 2000Ω (2K) resistance range, following a specific probe-color convention. A working sensor shows a proper, bounded resistance reading; a faulty sensor shows full-range resistance (0–10Ω) regardless of probe polarity/direction, indicating an internal potentiometer failure. Sensors should also buzz normally when 12V power is applied directly as an audio-indicator test 37.

    Non-OEM parking sensors failing specifically while driving. Aftermarket parking sensors (sourced from Ozon, Englingfly brand) showed a distinctive failure pattern: they beep correctly with the ignition on but the engine off, yet fail to beep or throw error codes during actual reverse driving. Root cause: substandard/counterfeit manufacturing with improper electrical resistance specs on the sensor elements. This affected multiple sellers and appears endemic to non-OEM sensors generally, not specific to one listing 38 3940414243. Conflict: some owners initially assumed the problem was specific to Ozon as a marketplace, but Victor clarified it's a broader non-OEM-sensor quality issue tied to resistance specs, not a platform-specific problem 4143.

    Working parking-sensor alternative: vedro.pro / KitZap. A confirmed working alternative sourcing option: seller warehouse POS276 (KitZap) on vedro.pro, part number 5GA179120 (FAW code), priced at roughly 1,590 RUB (RU market, May 2026). Reported functional by an owner who tested it; not claimed to be OEM-authentic, but performs correctly. An AliExpress listing at roughly 800 RUB (RU market, May 2026) was previously found but current availability there is unconfirmed 44454647.

    Warranty coverage for sensor replacement. When parking sensors show an error/fault display, the recommended approach: (1) get an ECU diagnostic session at an official FAW service center to identify which specific sensor failed; (2) if the vehicle is under 5 years old and under 100,000 km, the sensors are covered under warranty — pursue a warranty replacement rather than paying out of pocket. One owner successfully had all their damaged sensors replaced under warranty at no cost during the coverage period 48495051.

    Reliability pattern: intermittent errors become permanent, owners delay repair. Parking sensors are prone to intermittent errors that eventually become permanent failures 52. Once failed, owners tend to rely on the rear camera instead and delay sensor replacement indefinitely; front sensors in particular are often just worked around with visual estimation rather than repaired 53.
