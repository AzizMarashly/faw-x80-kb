    Fuel consumption reports by year and conditions

    Tags: #fuel-consumption · #buying-guide · #mkpp · #automatic-transmission

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    Community-reported fuel consumption varies substantially by model year, transmission type, engine load, and driving style: - 2019 facelift, manual (МКПП): ~10 L/100km city and highway (at 53k km mileage) 12. - 2018, manual: 8.2 L/100km city (at 110k km mileage) 3. - 2018 pre-facelift, manual: 7.1–7.3 L/100km reported in practice 45. - 2017, manual: 7.1–7.3 L/100km (238k km, tank not fully drained during test) 67. - Automatic (АКПП): 7.2–7.4 L/100km on highway with 4 passengers 89. - Highway trips at 90–100 km/h: 6.5–6.7 L/100km reported 1011. - A/C use and higher speeds increase consumption 1213. Some tests found 92-octane fuel gives lower consumption than 95 14. - Tank capacity: 50 liters; full-tank range 610–780 km depending on conditions 1516. Dashboard photo shows 5.6 L/100km at 33,005 km on one vehicle (media 11320).

    Later reports (November 2024 – April 2025) add further data points: on one vehicle with a touchscreen "lux" trim, fuel tank capacity is quoted as 58 liters with 11.6–11.8 L/100km typical automatic highway consumption; ECO mode is available but disliked by users ("annoying") 1718. A 2025-04-30 tracked trip of 410 km (70 km highway, rest city/mixed) showed 12.0 L/100km overall (≈12.6 city, ≈9 highway), attributed to aggressive acceleration; manual-transmission drivers report reducing city consumption to 11–11.5 L/100km with gentler driving 1920.

    July 2024, highway trip report (2000 km round-trip planning, Domodedovo–Lazarevskoe): For a roughly 3,600 km round-trip drive south, community estimates budgeted 5–6 full tanks of fuel 2122. Discussion emphasized tracking overall consumption per 100 km rather than the instantaneous readout, since the latter is misleading for trip budgeting 23. Regional fuel-quality notes: fill up before crossing into Crimea, since fuel there is reported as lower quality than on the mainland 24; in Krasnodar, the Teboil-branded station (formerly Shell) was rated good, while Rosneft fuel was considered suboptimal by at least one owner 25. A follow-up trip report from late July 2024 to Lazarevskoe, factoring in traffic delays, recorded 8 L/100km on the trip computer while sustaining 110–120 km/h on mountainous, winding roads — a reasonable efficiency result for the 2.0L engine at that pace 26.

    January 2026, winter cold-weather report: Driving from Tula in -11°C weather on 92-octane fuel, one owner recorded their lowest-ever fuel consumption reading, attributing it to gentle acceleration; they note they typically avoid exceeding ~140 km/h and that consumption crept up over time as driving habits became less conservative after the initial post-purchase period 27282930.

    August 2026, community poll on normal city/highway consumption: Asked what "normal" consumption looks like, owners reported a wide spread: 12.5 L/100km city (transmission not stated, highway not driven) 31; 12 L/100km city in summer, rising to 13 L/100km in winter (transmission not stated) 32; 9.5 L/100km summer city/highway combined 33; automatic transmission (АКПП) 10.5 L/100km city 34; and a highway trip of 8.4 L/100km over 640 km at a reduced ~100 km/h pace (driving without a roof box, amid a regional fuel shortage that slowed traffic generally) 35.

    September 2026, manual vs. automatic baseline at high mileage: One owner running a manual-transmission car at 215,000 km mileage reports 8-9 L/100km on the highway and 11-11.5 L/100km in the city, with no significant increase in consumption despite the high mileage 36. A separate owner shared a trip-computer photo (media 1903) captioned "here's my consumption on automatic" 37, illustrating that automatic-transmission cars in the community's reports tend to run somewhat higher than the manual figures above.
