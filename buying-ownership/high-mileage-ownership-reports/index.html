    High-mileage ownership reports (100k–400k+ km)

    Tags: #reliability · #buying-guide · #automatic-transmission

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Some advice here concerns sub-zero winters and is less relevant in a hot climate.

    Multiple community members report on the X80's long-term reliability at high mileage:

    • 170k–240k km: Owner purchased at 53k km in 2021, now at 170k km — reports good overall reliability, few factory defects, with most issues traced to STO (service station) improper maintenance rather than the vehicle itself 12. A second owner approaching 240k km had the transmission fluid changed at 200k km and reports smooth running 40k km later 34. A third owner (owner since Jan 2019, at 130k km) did a suspension refresh at 100k km, otherwise normal wear 5. General takeaway: reliability correlates more with owner understanding — gentle driving, preventive care, avoiding unnecessary tinkering — than with raw mileage; high-mileage owners in this thread suffered fewer issues than some 100k-km owners who had shop-induced problems 67.
    • 105k–200k km: Users report 105k km (2020 model) and 147k km (2019 model) with typical wear items — hub bearings, stabilizer links, disc/pad replacement 89. At 200k km with proper maintenance the mechanical drivetrain is reliable; however, robotized CVT transmissions are reported problematic, with failures reported around 100k km. Manual transmissions are reported as more durable 101112.
    • 400k+ km (automatic): One vehicle in service since May 2019 has passed 400,000 km on an automatic transmission with no reported oil-consumption issues. Community sentiment: the car has proven reliable contrary to early skepticism that the motor "wouldn't last 200k km" 131415. Odometer photo confirms 400,089 km (media 19714).
    • 245,000 km (mixed driving): Owner confirms galvanization visible on doors and hood; vehicle driven roughly 50% paved / 50% off-road (gravel, sand, clay, stumps, rough terrain) with no major rust and no oil-consumption issues 161718. Odometer photos confirm 245,782 km (media 19997, 19998).
    • 110,000 km (2.0 AT Max trim, 2021 model): Aggressive highway driving (repeated 180+ km/h cruising, 6000 rpm) plus 80% city aggressive driving (hard braking, rapid acceleration). Zero oil consumption up to 90,000 km; slight consumption began at 110,000 km in hot summer highway conditions (cause unclear — engine wear, poor dealer oil, or counterfeit oil). No misfires, smoke, or check-engine lights; instant cold starts; clean manual-mode shifting. Only oil, filters, brake pads, and discs were replaced over the full 110,000 km — the car "never failed" across Russian regions 1920.
    • 250,000+ km: Another owner accumulated 250,000+ km with only routine maintenance (oil, filters, pads, discs) and no major breakdowns; a separate owner reports the X80 tolerates any fuel quality and oil grade, though higher-quality products improve longevity 21222324.
    • 308,000 km (automatic): After an 11,000 km driving stretch the car consumed 300g of oil; suspension components wear faster on this vehicle due to varied terrain and speed. Reliability rated favorably against major brands; the car operates in temperatures down to -35°C. A partial transmission fluid change was done at 200k km with no issues; no oil leaks despite high mileage. Catalyst replacement (~306k km) and possible air-inlet valve work anticipated 25262728. Odometer photo confirms 308,000 km (media 37567).
    • 50k–163k km (five-owner survey, 2019–2020 models, Jan 2026): A 2020 model at 25,000 km reports excellent acceleration and road handling with only minor electrical glitches (sensor oxidation, intermittent ECU faults) 2930. A 2019 model at 58,000 km reports a comfortable interior suited to a 3-child family, full luxury package, stiffer-than-expected suspension (noted by an owner who switched from a Lada Vesta), and non-functional parktronic/TPMS sensors 313233. A second 2019 model at 82,000 km (purchased for 1.4M RUB, Jan 2026, RU market) reports good comfort and space, minor rust spots, a loud cabin (sound-dampening recommended), and mediocre stock audio 343536. Additional owners in the same thread report generally positive experiences up to 163,000 km, with issues limited to routine wear items 37383940414243444546.
    • 110,000 km (minimal service history): One owner reports 110,000 km with only an EGR valve replacement and no rust, contrasted against another owner at just 65,000 km who already experienced rust and drivability issues — maintenance history and unit-specific condition matter more than raw age or mileage 4748.
    • 320,000 km (owned 5+ years, purchased at 28,000 km): Engine described as robust; transmission has had only one half-drain with no complaints; no water ingress into the cabin; corrosion rare. Oil changed twice yearly (spring and fall). BCM (body control module) connector oxidation occurs regardless of water exposure 4950.
    • 200,000–400,000+ km (engine/transmission durability): Multiple owners report engines and transmissions (both manual and automatic) still functioning without major overhauls at very high mileage — one at 211,000 km (bought at 200,000 km, prior owner since fall 2021), another past 300,000 km. Regular maintenance remains critical, but the drivetrain itself is reported as durable long-term 51525354.
    • 300,000–400,000+ km ("workhorses"): Community reports vehicles in this range still running reliably with regular oil/filter changes and preventive care; post-restyling models are particularly valued in the secondary market despite some known design flaws 5556.
    • 165,000–202,000+ km (multiple owners): One owner at 165,000 km replaced the front hub bearing and an engine mount; another at 100,000 km changed only the front hub bearing; one at 180,000 km changed brake pads, a wheel bearing, and lamps; one reports 202,000+ km in ongoing daily use, calling the car reliable ("hasn't let me down since 2020"), comfortable, with good clearance and decent power. Common complaints across all mileages: interior squeaks/creaks and a transmission jerk on takeoff from rest 57585960616263.
    • 105,000 km / 5.5 years: Only one significant electrical failure over the ownership period — a wiring-harness short circuit under the driver's seat — otherwise the electrical system has been reliable. Owner recommends professional diagnostics over trial-and-error troubleshooting for non-obvious electrical faults 64.
    • 237,000 km (2017 model, purchased new July 2018, as of 2024-09-02): Only the rear hub assembly was replaced; the automatic transmission fluid received a partial change at 200,000 km (oil did not darken, appeared normal); no engine work and no oil consumption on Mobil-brand oil. Suspension components (lower arms, bushings, struts, links) needed refresh at this mileage — described as normal wear for the X80. Overall assessment: reliable, resourceful car that drives well despite its age 656667686970.
    • 177,000–420,000 km (community confirmation): One member reports an X80 with 177,000 km still in operation; others in a related WhatsApp group report vehicles at 350,000–420,000 km, reinforcing the pattern of long-term durability potential 7172.
    • 181,000 km (manual transmission, 6 years): Only the clutch has needed replacement (just starting to feel stiff when shifting); the rest of the drivetrain has been completely reliable despite hard use — 4 accidents, rough off-road driving, and gravel roads. Owner's verdict: "extremely durable vehicle" 7374.
    • 193,000 km (survived 4 collisions): Owner reports the car remains roadworthy and satisfying to drive despite the accident history and high mileage, and explicitly does not plan to sell it — cited as a real-world durability test 75.
    • 2017 model, four years in (Feb 2024): owner reports the car is "all great" entering its fourth year of ownership, with no specific issues volunteered 76.
    • 2020 model, one year of ownership (May 2025): zero problems in the first year; the owner has not personally faced a parts-sourcing issue and cannot speak to that concern from direct experience; highway-stable at 160 km/h and possibly beyond; average fuel consumption 7–8 L/100km on the highway when driven gently; overall comfort assessment is relative to whatever car the buyer is switching from 77.
    • 2019 restyle, 90,000 km, bought roughly two months earlier for 1,150,000 RUB (Dec 2025, RU market): rock-stable at highway speed with plenty of interior space; parts needs so far limited to an upper control arm (sourced via Ozon, Mazda 6-compatible, 2,700 RUB, direct fit) and stabilizer links (also Mazda-sourced, found in stock locally); leather interior in good condition; the car looks new enough that acquaintances are shocked to learn it is a six-year-old Chinese import; fuel consumption 8 L/100km highway in eco mode, 11.5–13 L/100km city depending on driving style 78798081.
    • 2020 model, 41,000 km, two years of ownership (Dec 2025): owner reports being "very satisfied in all respects" — interior, exterior, and technical specifications; has the financial means to buy a new car but cannot bring himself to part with this one, since nothing currently on the market matches its specification for the price; rock-stable at 160 km/h; fuel consumption 8 L/100km highway (calm driving), 10–12 L/100km city; only complaint is a long winter warm-up before the windshield defroster clears frozen glass (unclear whether unique to this unit); the car is rare enough in traffic to draw attention and stand out 82838485.
