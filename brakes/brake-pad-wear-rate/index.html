    Brake pad wear rate — front vs rear

    Tags: #brake-pad · #wear · #front-axle · #rear-axle

    Prices and suppliers on this page are Russian-market (RUB, at the dates shown). Verify locally before relying on them in Syria.

    Front brake pads wear roughly 2x faster than rear pads, attributed to weight transfer during braking (front axle carries ~70% of braking load, rear ~30%). Typical owner-reported replacement intervals: front pads ~40,000 km, rear pads ~60,000–80,000 km. Community consensus is that front pads should wear faster under normal braking.1234567

    Conflict: a minority of owners report the opposite — rear pads wearing faster — possibly due to wear-indicator quality differences or driving style.1

    Separately, in aggressive urban driving several owners quantify this as roughly 2 front pad changes per 1 rear pad change. Rear pads on the X80 have no wear sensor, so rear wear must be checked manually/visually rather than via a dashboard warning. Pad life overall is highly dependent on driving style — aggressive braking accelerates wear on both axles.891011121314

    Conflict: one owner in this second discussion also reported the opposite wear pattern (rear wearing faster), again likely explained by individual driving style.8

    Price vs. lifespan trade-off

    Following on from the same discussion: premium pads cost roughly 2x the price of budget pads but deliver roughly 2x the lifespan, so the per-kilometer cost is similar — the main practical difference is service frequency, not total spend. Typical full lifespan for quality pads: 100,000–150,000 km before they're worn out. Cheaper pads (around 8,000 RUB in one reported case, RU market, June 2025) tend to fail quickly, while higher-priced quality pads are seen as justified by the extended service interval.15

    Conflict: OEM pads wearing much faster than the ~40,000 km norm (independent report)

    A separate community group reports OEM/factory pads are not special and can wear out far faster than the ~40,000 km typical figure documented above: one owner's OEM pads lasted only ~20,000 km, with rapid rotor wear alongside them — roughly half the expected front-pad life. Community advice from this thread leans toward accepting OEM parts and the associated maintenance cost regardless, since aftermarket performance also varies.16171819 Conflict: this ~20,000 km OEM-pad lifespan directly contradicts the ~40,000 km front / 60,000–80,000 km rear figures reported above from the larger discussion — likely explained by the same driving-style variance already noted as a source of disagreement in this card, though it could also reflect a batch-quality or model-year difference in the OEM pads themselves.
